The rule that stops it being an interview

Why a ladder and not a list

Most first-date question lists fail in the same way: every question on them is a rung three question handed to someone standing on the ground. "What is your biggest regret" is a good question in the right place, and in the first ten minutes it is an ambush. So each topic here has three rungs, and the rung matters more than the question.

RungWhat it doesWhen it is wrong
One: the openerEasy to answer, impossible to get wrong, tells you whether the topic is hers at allTwenty minutes in, when you have both warmed up and it reads as treading water
Two: the specific oneNarrows to a detail, which is where people start sounding like themselvesBefore rung one has landed. A specific question about a topic she does not care about is worse than a dull one
Three: the storyAsks for a scene rather than an answer, and gives her something to performCold. This is the rung that makes people feel interrogated if the first two were skipped

The pattern in the right-hand column is always the same: it asks for one concrete thing rather than a category, and it is slightly easier to answer honestly than impressively.

The three ways a first date conversation dies

You interview her. Questions with nothing of yours in between. She ends up doing all the work and reads the whole thing as an assessment. The fix is in the tool above and it is not optional: answer your own question.

You climb too fast. A story-level question in the first five minutes gets a polite, short, careful answer, and then the topic is spent. Rung one costs you nothing and tells you whether rung three is even there.

You never land the next thing. The conversation goes well, nobody says anything concrete, and it ends on "we should do this again" which means nothing. Naming a specific thing, with a day in it, is a separate skill from talking, and it is the one most people skip. Asking her out, in detail.

Questions

Am I supposed to memorise these?

No. Learn the shape instead: easy question, then the specific version of it, then the one that asks for a story. Once you can hear which rung you are on, you can build the ladder live out of whatever she actually says, which is always better than anything prepared.

Will this not sound like an interview?

It will if you only ask. The rule that stops it is simple and it is not optional: answer your own question before she has to ask you back. One question, her answer, then your version of the same answer. That is a conversation. Question, answer, next question is an interview, and three of those in a row is when a date dies.

What if she gives a one-word answer?

Do not climb. A short answer means the rung was too high or the topic is not hers, so go sideways to a different topic rather than pushing for more on that one. Pushing is what turns a flat answer into a flat date.
