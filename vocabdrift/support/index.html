Vocabdrift, explained

/ˈvoʊ.kæb.drɪft/   noun

A word and a short definition, on the Lock Screen, rotating on a schedule you set — plus a quiz that asks you to produce them back. Free, no account, no network.

Help page last revised 3 November 2026

Almost everything people write in about is one of three things: the widget not showing up in the picker, the word not changing exactly on the hour, or a suspicion that something must cost money. All three are answered below, in that order.

The widgetGetting a word onto the Lock Screen

The widget isn’t in the lock-screen picker.
This is a known iOS quirk with newly installed apps — restart your phone once and Vocabdrift will appear under Customize. It’s not something you did wrong.
How do I add the widget?
Press and hold your lock screen, tap Customize, tap the row below the time, and pick Vocabdrift. The app’s onboarding walks you through it with pictures.
Is there a Home Screen or StandBy widget?
No. Vocabdrift ships one widget, for the rectangular slot on the Lock Screen, and nothing else. That is a deliberate limit rather than an omission: the whole idea is the screen you already look at dozens of times a day without unlocking anything.
How often does the word change?
Whatever you set in the app’s Settings: every 4, 8, 12 or 24 hours. iOS refreshes lock-screen widgets on its own timing, so Vocabdrift loads a long run of upcoming words in advance — the widget plays them back without needing to fetch anything.
Why isn’t the word updating exactly when I expect?
iOS controls when lock-screen widgets refresh and limits how often. Vocabdrift precomputes the schedule so it stays close, but the system has the final say on timing.
Does it need internet?
No. Your words are stored on your device and rotate offline. There’s no account and no server call to see them.

The quizWhy glancing isn’t the same as knowing

What’s the quiz for?
Glancing at a word on the Lock Screen helps you recognize it; the active-recall quiz makes you retrieve it, which is what actually moves it into memory.
Where do the quiz questions come from?
From words the widget has already shown you, so you are never asked to produce something you have not met. The wrong answers are drawn from the rest of the word list.
Why did a word I already knew come back in the quiz?
The quiz weights words you’ve missed before, so they come up more often until you answer them right. Once you do, they stop getting the extra weight.
Is this spaced repetition?
Not in the strict sense, and the app does not claim to be. There are no scheduled intervals and no review dates. Missed words simply carry more weight in the draw than words you have not missed.
Can I focus on just certain words?
Yes — star any word on the Home screen to favorite it. Once you’ve starred at least 4, you can quiz yourself on just your favorites.
Can I look back at words I’ve already seen?
Yes. The Home screen has a “Words you’ve seen” link that opens the full history, newest first, with a star on each row. You can also read ahead: the next six words queued for your Lock Screen are listed on the Home screen.

Your wordsWhere everything lives

Vocabdrift has no account and no server, so the honest answer to “where is my data” is a short table rather than a paragraph.

Your progressOn the handset only, in the app’s own storage, which iOS is told to keep out of your device backup. A restore to a new phone therefore starts it fresh.
Starred wordsSame place, same rule.
The word scheduleIn the file the widget reads, which is in your device backup. It holds upcoming words and dates — nothing you typed, no results, no favourites.
Anything on our sideNothing. There is no server to hold a copy in.

The privacy policy says the same thing at length, including what was measured on a running build rather than assumed.

Where’s my progress stored?
All on your device. Vocabdrift has no account and no server, so your progress and starred words stay on your phone and never come to us. They’re in your iCloud backup if that’s enabled, and deleting the app clears them.
Does my progress sync across devices?
Not directly — because Vocabdrift keeps everything on-device with no account, progress lives on the phone you use rather than syncing between devices. The upside is total privacy; the tradeoff is no live cross-device sync. If your devices share an iCloud backup, a restore carries data over, but it won’t sync live between them.

The word listWhere the definitions come from

Who wrote the definitions?
Nobody here. They come from WordNet 3.0 (Princeton University) and Wiktionary, under those projects’ own open licences, with the attribution shown inside the app. The list that ships is 1,383 words, each with a plain definition and its part of speech; 1,165 of them carry a written IPA pronunciation and 535 carry a real usage example. The app has no audio.
Why does one word have an example sentence and the next one doesn’t?
Because an example is only included when it genuinely belongs to the same sense as the definition shown and actually contains the headword. A sentence that illustrates a different sense of the same word is worse than no sentence, so those are dropped rather than padded in. That is why the count is 535 and not 1,383.
A definition or word is wrong.
Thanks for catching it — we’d rather know. Definitions come from a curated set, and mistakes do slip through. Tell us the exact word and what looks off, and we’ll verify it and fix it in an update.

MoneyThere isn’t any

Is there a Pro version or anything to buy?
No. Vocabdrift is entirely free — no subscription, no in-app purchase, no ads, nothing to unlock.
So there is nothing to cancel, restore or refund?
Correct. Nothing in this app can charge you, which means there is no subscription to cancel, no purchase to restore and no refund to request. If that ever changes, this page will describe the real steps before the change ships, not after.

Something’s wrongReporting it

Something’s broken. How do I report it?
Email us with: (1) device and OS version, (2) what you were doing (adding the widget, a quiz, browsing words…), (3) expected vs. actual, and (4) a screenshot.
Can I ask for a word to be added?
Yes, and those requests are the easiest ones to act on. Send the word; if it is in WordNet or Wiktionary with a sense that survives the checks above, it can go into the next list update.
