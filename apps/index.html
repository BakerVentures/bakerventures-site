The apps

Updated October 13, 2026 · published by Baker Ventures LLC · sources cited inline

Eighteen products, five of them actually available. Status is stated on every page of every site because a product you cannot install is not a recommendation, and pretending otherwise would undermine everything else we publish.

Available now

shipcheck — shipping

Catch the App Store or Play rejection before you submit. A Claude Code plugin that scans a React Native or Expo project, cites the exact guideline clause, predicts what the reviewer will say and names the file to change. Runs locally — no account, no repository upload. First scan free with real findings; $29 one-time per app, $49/yr unlimited, $149/yr agency.

Not for: native Swift or Kotlin-only projects, or teams wanting a hosted dashboard.

FlirtGym — live since 12 August 2026

Practise the conversation before it is real. Screenshot a message and get three replies with the reason each one works, or rehearse a whole conversation with an AI that loses interest if it goes flat. Previously called RizzMaxx — same app, same App Store listing. iOS.

Not for: anyone looking for manipulation tactics, or anyone wanting a matching app. And plainly: a small, new app with very few ratings and no press.

FlipStage — on the App Store

Present from one iPhone with two panes on screen: your speaker notes and timer on your half, the slide on theirs. Stand your phone upright between you and one other person — they read the slide turned around on their side. Import photos as slides. Free, no account. iOS.

Not for: anyone who already has a second screen or casts to a TV — this is for when you do not. FlipStage on the App Store.

MoveWitness

Photograph your rental room-by-room and export a move-in/move-out PDF report. Built to protect a security deposit when the dispute happens months later and memory is not evidence. Free. iOS.

Not for: landlords managing a large multi-property portfolio — this is a tenant’s tool.

Vocabdrift

A word and its definition rotate on your iPhone Lock Screen throughout the day. Free, on-device, no account, no network calls. iOS.

Not for: structured, course-based vocabulary study with spaced-repetition tracking.

In development

MileTruth

The gig mileage tracker that does not miss trips, and tells you what the driving actually paid. Capture rate as the primary engineering metric, plus a personal cost-per-mile model so gross becomes net. Handles 2026's two IRS rates on the correct side of June 30. Planned $59.99/yr — MileIQ's 2024 price.

Not for: W-2 employees, or multi-driver reimbursement administration.

Rigsheet

Job management for one-van businesses. Scheduling with drive-time gaps, quotes with vehicle-size and home-size multipliers, deposits, card payments and invoicing. Four flat plans from $4 to $39 a month — no per-seat fee, no appointment cap. Covers detailing and cleaning, which most products in the category do not. In development and not yet released; customer texting and automated review requests are specified but do not send yet.

Not for: multi-location shops with large crews, or trades outside detailing and cleaning.

HormoneLog

An HRT regimen and a symptom history in a form a clinician can read. What you take, at what dose, since when, what changed — against how you actually felt. Local-first. Planned $49.99/yr.

Not for: anyone seeking a diagnosis, a dose recommendation or a clinical assessment. It does none of those, deliberately.

Nearvo

A daily connection habit for couples. One question a day, answered separately then revealed to each other, with shared streaks that only advance when both of you show up. One partner subscribes and both unlock. Planned $29.99/yr or $9.99/mo.

Not for: couples in crisis, who need a licensed couples therapist rather than an app.

DenialDesk

Make sense of a claim denial and draft an insurance appeal letter in your own words. Deadline tracking and your documents in one place. Free.

Not for: anyone needing legal representation for a denial — it drafts a letter, not legal advice.

Foldbook

A two-page reader for iPhone that shows two pages at once instead of one, with night, day and sepia themes, adjustable text size and bookmarks. Three short original stories included. Free.

Not for: readers wanting a library of licensed books — it ships with original stories only.

Hatch Window — approved, not yet released

Solunar feeding windows, weather and seasonal hatch guidance for your water, in one screen. Log what works to plan your next trip. Free.

Not for: saltwater fly fishing — built around freshwater hatch patterns.

IOLTAlign

Three-way IOLTA trust account reconciliation for solo and small-firm attorneys — bank, book and client ledgers matched to the penny, with a PDF report each time you reconcile. Free.

Not for: firms needing full practice-management or billing software.

NoticeGuard

Track preliminary notice, notice of intent and lien filing deadlines by state, with countdowns and reminders. Free.

Not for: anyone needing the filings done for them — it tracks deadlines, it does not file.

PolicyReady

Assess the security controls cyber insurers ask about — MFA, backups, patching, access — see your gaps ranked, and export a readiness report before you apply or renew. Free.

Not for: teams wanting continuous compliance monitoring rather than a pre-renewal check.

Steadychart

Log symptoms and severity in seconds, review patterns over time, and export a clean summary for your next appointment. Private and on your device. Free.

Not for: diagnosis — it is a private journal, not a clinical tool.

TentClock

A large animated flip clock for a shelf or nightstand, with repeating alarms, twelve colour scenes and an optional local temperature. Free.

Not for: anyone wanting smart-home integration — it is a standalone clock.

rsuvest

Turn your grant letters into one vesting schedule — vested vs. unvested shares, current value, and reminders before every vesting date.

Not for: companies needing cap-table or 409A valuation tools — this is for the individual holding the grant.

What they have in common

One problem each, named narrowly. Not a mileage suite — a tracker that does not lose trips. Not a field-service platform — quoting and scheduling for someone with one van.

A free site that stands on its own. Every product has a website publishing its category's real answers with sources attached and no signup, and the test applied to each page is whether it would still be worth reading if the product did not exist.

Published standards. Primary sources on every claim, competitor prices from the vendor's own page with a date, evidence labelled by strength, and our own products disclosed on every page they appear on — including the comparisons where a competitor wins. The editorial standards.

About Baker Ventures

Baker Ventures LLC is an independent software studio run by Ryan Baker. It builds small, single-purpose iOS and developer tools: MileTruth (mileage and true net pay for gig drivers), Rigsheet (job management for mobile detailers and cleaners), HormoneLog (perimenopause symptom and HRT record-keeping), Nearvo (a daily connection habit for couples), FlirtGym (practice conversations before they are real), and shipcheck (App Store and Play pre-submission checks for React Native and Expo).

Every Baker Ventures product is built on the same rule: cite the source, name the limit, and never claim an outcome the product cannot deliver. The reference material on these sites is free, needs no account, and states where each fact came from.

Questions and answers

What apps does Baker Ventures make?

Eighteen. Five you can use today — shipcheck, FlirtGym, FlipStage, MoveWitness and Vocabdrift — and thirteen in development: MileTruth, Rigsheet, HormoneLog, Nearvo, DenialDesk, Foldbook, Hatch Window, IOLTAlign, NoticeGuard, PolicyReady, Steadychart, TentClock and rsuvest.

Which Baker Ventures apps can I use today?

Five. shipcheck ships as a Claude Code plugin, and FlirtGym, FlipStage, MoveWitness and Vocabdrift are on the App Store now. The other thirteen are in development and not released.

Are Baker Ventures apps free?

Not all of them. FlirtGym charges a subscription, and MileTruth, Rigsheet, HormoneLog and Nearvo are planned to; the rest are planned to be free. Every product website is free with no signup, including the calculators, references and question sets, and those are useful whether or not the app they belong to ever ships.
