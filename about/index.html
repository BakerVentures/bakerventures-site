About Baker Ventures

Updated October 14, 2026 · published by Baker Ventures LLC · sources cited inline

Baker Ventures LLC is a one-person independent software studio. It builds small, single-purpose iOS apps and developer tools, and it publishes free, source-linked answers for each of their categories.

Being straight about the size: six of nineteen products are actually available. shipcheck ships as a Claude Code plugin, and FlirtGym, FlipStage, Hatch Window, MoveWitness and Vocabdrift are on the App Store. The other thirteen are listed on the apps page with the status of each one. Every page about an unreleased app says so.

That is relevant context rather than modesty. It tells you what to expect from support, from release cadence, and from a roadmap.

No release dates are published anywhere on this site. An app that is waiting on Apple goes on sale by itself the moment Apple approves it, Apple decides when that is, and it does not tell us in advance.

The pattern

Every one of them is the same shape: one problem, named narrowly, solved without a platform around it.

Not a mileage suite — a log that shows a gig driver what the shift actually paid after the car takes its cut. Not practice-management software — a three-way trust reconciliation that ties three ledgers to the penny, or shows exactly where they do not. Not a sleep platform — a flip clock for a shelf. Not a health platform — a record of what you take and how you feel, in a form a clinician can read.

The consistent design constraint is saying what a thing is not for. Every product page here has a "who this is not for" section, because a product that claims to suit everyone is describing a category rather than a tool.

The second constraint is that a claim has to be true of the build you can install, not the one in the repository and not the next version. Where a feature is missing, the page says so.

Why the websites give everything away

Each product has a site that publishes its category's real answers, free, with sources attached and no signup: the two-rate 2026 mileage calculator, the required-reason API reference, the job pricing floor calculator, the question sets, the menopause symptom-scale explainers.

Two reasons.

The answers are useful whether or not the apps ship. Some of these products may never launch. The two-rate calculator still works, and the person who needed it still got it.

A page that only makes sense as an advert is not worth writing. The test applied to every page is whether it would still be worth reading if the product did not exist. Most of them would.

Open data

The Apple privacy-manifest SDK → npm map — all 86 SDKs on Apple's third-party requirements list mapped to the React Native and Expo packages that pull each one in, with the five required-reason API categories and all sixteen approved reason codes transcribed from Apple's rendered documentation. CC BY 4.0, corrections welcome as issues or pull requests.

Published openly because it is the kind of thing that should exist and did not, and because a dataset other people can correct stays accurate longer than one that only we can edit.

How the writing works

Everything published here follows one set of rules, which are themselves published so they can be held against us: primary sources on every claim, competitor prices from the vendor's own page with a date, no number published if it cannot be verified at source, evidence labelled by its strength, and our own products disclosed on every page they appear on — including the pages where a competitor is the better answer.
