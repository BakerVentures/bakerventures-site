What the integrity digest is — and is not

Every report the app generates carries a SHA-256 integrity digest computed over the report's own contents: the property label, the inspection's identifiers and timestamps, each room's name, notes and condition, and the actual bytes of every photo included. Regenerate the report from unchanged data and you get the same digest; change a note, swap a photo, or drop a room and the digest changes. That is what it is for — making a silent edit detectable.

What it is not, in the app's own words, printed in that same footer:

A content digest, not a signature — it does not prove when this file was created.

Specifically, the digest is not a cryptographic signature, not a notarisation, and not a trusted third-party timestamp. It is computed on your own device, from data on your own device, with no key only we hold and no external timestamp authority involved. It therefore proves nothing to someone who does not trust that device or its clock, and it does not establish authenticity, authorship, or the time of anything. Do not present it as a signature or as proof of when a photograph was taken. (The app does read and store each photo's own EXIF capture date where the photo has one, but that metadata comes from the file you supplied and we do not verify it.)

Completing an inspection locks it: once completed, no room, note, or photo on that inspection can be added, edited, or removed through the app. That lock is a feature of this app's own data layer on your own device — it is a discipline that makes your record more credible, not a legal guarantee that no one could ever alter data by other means.
