Privacy Policy — NoticeGuard

Last updated: September 23, 2026

NoticeGuard is built and operated by Baker Ventures LLC. This policy explains exactly what the app stores, what it does with it, and who else can see it.

The short version

NoticeGuard does not collect, transmit, sell, or share any personal data. There is no account, no sign-in, and no server for this app to talk to. Everything you enter stays in a database on your own device.

What the app stores, and why

All of it is saved only in a local database on your device. Nothing here is uploaded.

  • Job name — free text you type, used to label the job in your list and in an exported calendar entry. The field suggests a format like a street address, so what ends up here is whatever you choose to write; it is stored exactly as typed. Nothing reads it, geocodes it, or looks it up.
  • State or jurisdiction for the job — selects which statutory rule set to apply.
  • Your role on the job (original contractor, subcontractor, supplier, or preconstruction service provider) — selects which deadlines apply to you.
  • Job dates you enter, such as first and last furnishing, completion, or recording dates — the anchors every deadline is counted from.
  • A small number of yes/no facts about the project, such as whether it is a single-family dwelling — these select between branches of a statute.
  • Created/updated timestamps on each job, so the app can order your list.

What the app never collects

No account or sign-in. No name, email address, phone number, or postal address field. No party names, property descriptions, or dollar amounts — where a statutory document needs those, the app leaves the blank for you to fill in yourself rather than storing them. No location, camera, microphone, photo library, contacts, or calendar access. No advertising identifier. No analytics SDK and no crash-reporting SDK — the app does not include one.

Third parties

There are none. NoticeGuard makes no network requests of any kind, so there is no service — ours or anyone else's — receiving your data. Nothing is sold, rented, or shared, and no data is used for advertising or tracking, because none of it ever leaves your device.

Statute citations

Every rule and document in the app carries the citation and the source web address it was read from, shown as plain text with the date it was retrieved. Those addresses are printed for your reference only — the app does not open them, does not fetch them, and has no in-app browser. Nothing is requested from a legislature's website while you use the app.

Reminders

If you turn on reminders, the app schedules local notifications ahead of your deadlines. They are composed and delivered entirely on your device by iOS or Android — there is no push server and no push token. Because a reminder names the job, it may be visible on your lock screen; turn reminders off in your device settings if you would rather it not be.

Calendar export

The calendar (.ics) export writes a file to your device and hands it to the system share sheet. The file contains your job names and computed deadline dates. Where it goes after that is entirely your choice — the app does not upload it anywhere, and we never receive a copy. Anything you then send through another app is governed by that app's privacy policy, not this one.

Keeping, deleting, and revoking

Your data is kept on your device until you remove it — we have no copy and no retention period to apply. Deleting a job removes it and everything stored with it immediately. Deleting the app removes the entire database; there is no account or server copy to also delete, and no request you need to send us. You can revoke notification permission at any time in your device settings without losing any saved data.

Children

NoticeGuard is a professional tool for contractors, subcontractors, and suppliers, is not directed at children, and collects no personal data from anyone, of any age.

Not legal advice

This app reports what the statute says and cites the section it read. It is not legal advice, it does not file anything for you, and it does not tell you what to do.

Statutes change, and courts reinterpret them. Every deadline in this app comes from a statute read on the date shown next to its citation — that snapshot can go out of date. Missing a mechanics lien deadline can extinguish your claim permanently, so confirm any deadline you are relying on with an attorney licensed in that state before you act on it.

Changes to this policy

If what the app stores or contacts ever changes, this page is updated in the same app update that makes the change — never afterwards.
