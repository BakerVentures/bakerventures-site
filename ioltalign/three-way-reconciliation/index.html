Three-Way Trust Reconciliation: Printable Checklist

A three-way reconciliation compares an adjusted bank balance, the trust account book balance and the total of individual client or matter ledgers at the same cutoff date. A matching total is one check; it does not establish compliance or prove that each entry is correct.

Not legal or accounting advice. Your jurisdiction’s rules and professional duties govern your process, records and frequency. This general checklist cannot certify compliance. Consult your bar’s trust-accounting resources or a qualified adviser.

The three balances

  1. Adjusted bank balance. Start with the statement ending balance. Add deposits in transit and subtract outstanding payments after checking the supporting records. Investigate other bank adjustments separately.
  2. Trust book balance. Use the account’s recorded receipts and disbursements through the same cutoff. Investigate missing entries and bank fees rather than silently forcing a match.
  3. Client ledger total. Sum the individual ledger balances at that cutoff. Review each balance as well as the total; offsetting errors can disappear in a sum.

Illustrative arithmetic

Sample only, fictional amounts: a statement balance of 12,000 plus 1,000 in deposits in transit, less 500 in outstanding payments, produces an adjusted bank balance of 12,500. The book and client ledger totals must each be compared with that 12,500. This example is not a certification or a description of a real account.

When the totals differ

Check the cutoff first, then transposed amounts, duplicates, missing entries, bank charges and outstanding items. Do not create a balancing entry without identifying and documenting its basis. Follow your professional escalation process for unexplained differences.
