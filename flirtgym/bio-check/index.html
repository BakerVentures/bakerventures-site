Every right-hand example here is invented for illustration. The point is the shape of the sentence, not the content: one named thing, one admission, and something a stranger can reply to without thinking hard.

The three-second test

Read your bio and ask what somebody could write to you using only what is in it. If the honest answer is "hey, how's your week going", the bio did not do its job, regardless of how well written it is. A bio is not a description of you. It is a set of handles.

This is also why the scoring weights Specific and Answerable most heavily. A charming bio with no handles produces polite nothing. A plain bio with two handles produces conversations, and conversations are the only thing any of this is for.

Questions

Is my bio uploaded anywhere?

No. The scoring is JavaScript running on your device. There is no request, no logging, no cookie and no storage, and the page works with your connection turned off.

How long should a bio be?

Fifteen to sixty words scores best here, and the reason is mechanical rather than aesthetic: under fifteen words there is usually nothing concrete in it, and over about a hundred it stops being read before the good part. The number is not the point. One specific thing plus one way in is, and that normally lands in that range on its own.

Why does it penalise phrases that are true about me?

Because a stock phrase is true about almost everyone, which is what makes it useless rather than untrue. If a sentence would be just as true about the next forty profiles, it is taking up space without telling anyone anything. Swap it for the version only you could have written.

Does a better bio matter more than better photos?

No, and this page will not pretend otherwise. Photos are looked at first and decide whether the text is read at all. The bio decides what happens next, which is the part people actually get stuck on, and it is also the part you can rewrite in five minutes at no cost.
