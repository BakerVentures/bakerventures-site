What hatches in April: flies and patterns

10 entries in the Hatch Window curated chart are typically active in April, with the fly patterns that match each.

By water type

  • Tailwater: Midges, Blue-Winged Olive, Caddisflies (general), Baitfish / sculpins (streamer season), Scuds & Sowbugs
  • Freestone: Midges, Blue-Winged Olive, Skwala Stonefly, Caddisflies (general), Baitfish / sculpins (streamer season), Hendrickson, Grannom Caddis, Quill Gordon, Blue Quill
  • Spring creek: Midges, Blue-Winged Olive, Caddisflies (general), Baitfish / sculpins (streamer season), Hendrickson, Grannom Caddis, Scuds & Sowbugs, Blue Quill

Hatches and matching patterns

Midges

Chironomidae
Typical months: January to December. Water types: Tailwater, Freestone, Spring creek. Regions: Mountain West; Northeast (Catskills/Appalachians); Midwest (Driftless/Great Lakes); South (Southern Appalachians/Southeast tailwaters).
  • Zebra Midge (nymph, size 18-22)
  • Griffith's Gnat (dry, size 18-22)
  • Rojo Midge (nymph, size 20-24)

Year-round on tailwaters, most reliable hatch on cold winter days when little else is moving.

Blue-Winged Olive

Baetis spp.
Typical months: February to April, September to November. Water types: Tailwater, Freestone, Spring creek. Regions: Mountain West; Northeast (Catskills/Appalachians); Midwest (Driftless/Great Lakes); South (Southern Appalachians/Southeast tailwaters).
  • Parachute Adams (dry, size 16-20)
  • Pheasant Tail Nymph (nymph, size 16-20)
  • CDC BWO Sparkle Dun (emerger, size 18-20)

Best on overcast, drizzly spring and fall days; spring and fall are two distinct emergence windows.

Skwala Stonefly

Skwala spp.
Typical months: February to April. Water types: Freestone. Regions: Mountain West.
  • Skwala Stone Dry (dry, size 8-12)
  • Pat's Rubber Legs (nymph, size 8-10)

Early-season Western freestone stonefly; fish move to banks well before the well-known salmonfly hatch.

Caddisflies (general)

Trichoptera
Typical months: April to August. Water types: Tailwater, Freestone, Spring creek. Regions: Mountain West; Northeast (Catskills/Appalachians); Midwest (Driftless/Great Lakes); South (Southern Appalachians/Southeast tailwaters).
  • Elk Hair Caddis (dry, size 14-18)
  • Sparkle Pupa (emerger, size 14-18)
  • LaFontaine's Deep Sparkle Pupa (nymph, size 14-16)

The spring "Mother's Day caddis" emergence is a marquee Western event on rivers like the Arkansas.

Baitfish / sculpins (streamer season)

Typical months: March to April, October to November. Water types: Tailwater, Freestone, Spring creek. Regions: Mountain West; Northeast (Catskills/Appalachians); Midwest (Driftless/Great Lakes); South (Southern Appalachians/Southeast tailwaters).
  • Woolly Bugger (streamer, size 4-10)
  • Sculpzilla (streamer, size 2-6)

Not a hatch, fished when bugs are scarce and big fish are feeding aggressively (pre-runoff and fall, ahead of brown trout spawning).

Hendrickson

Ephemerella subvaria
Typical months: April to May. Water types: Freestone, Spring creek. Regions: Northeast (Catskills/Appalachians).
  • Hendrickson Dry (Catskill-tie) (dry, size 12-14)
  • Red Quill (dry, size 12-14)
  • Hare's Ear Nymph (nymph, size 12-16)

The classic early-season Catskill hatch, the pattern that launched American dry-fly tying on rivers like the Beaverkill and Willowemoc.

Grannom Caddis

Brachycentrus spp.
Typical months: April to May. Water types: Freestone, Spring creek. Regions: Northeast (Catskills/Appalachians); Midwest (Driftless/Great Lakes); South (Southern Appalachians/Southeast tailwaters).
  • Black Caddis (Elk Hair) (dry, size 16-18)
  • Green Weenie (nymph, size 12-14)
  • Deep Sparkle Pupa (dark) (emerger, size 16-18)

An early-spring blizzard hatch on Eastern and Midwest freestones/spring creeks, often nicknamed the "apple caddis" for coinciding with apple blossoms.

Scuds & Sowbugs

Gammarus / Asellus spp.
Typical months: January to December. Water types: Tailwater, Spring creek. Regions: Mountain West; Northeast (Catskills/Appalachians); Midwest (Driftless/Great Lakes); South (Southern Appalachians/Southeast tailwaters).
  • Scud (olive/tan) (nymph, size 14-18)
  • Sowbug (nymph, size 14-18)

Not a hatch, a year-round staple on cold, stable tailwaters and spring creeks (the South Holston and Clinch in Tennessee, the Bighorn and San Juan out West, Pennsylvania limestoners, the Driftless Area) where crustaceans outnumber insects.

Quill Gordon

Epeorus pleuralis
Typical months: February to April. Water types: Freestone. Regions: Northeast (Catskills/Appalachians); South (Southern Appalachians/Southeast tailwaters).
  • Quill Gordon Dry (Catskill-tie) (dry, size 12-14)
  • Quill Gordon Soft Hackle (emerger, size 12-14)
  • Hare's Ear Nymph (nymph, size 12-16)

One of the first major spring mayflies on Southern Appalachian (Great Smoky Mountains) and Catskill freestones; emerges midday in cold water, nymphs cling to fast riffles.

Blue Quill

Paraleptophlebia adoptiva
Typical months: March to April. Water types: Freestone, Spring creek. Regions: Northeast (Catskills/Appalachians); South (Southern Appalachians/Southeast tailwaters).
  • Blue Quill Parachute (dry, size 16-18)
  • Pheasant Tail Nymph (nymph, size 16-18)

A small, dark early-spring mayfly that overlaps the Quill Gordon in the Smokies and the Northeast; smaller and later into the afternoon than the Gordon.
