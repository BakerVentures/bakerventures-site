The one adjustment

The two axes, and why these two

This is a self-assessment of habits, not a personality test, and it does not claim to measure who you are. It measures two things you actually do, chosen because they are the two that moved the numbers in a study of 80 scored openers.

In that study, tone was almost never the problem. Even generic greetings scored 4.6 out of 10 on tone. What collapsed was specificity and momentum, both scoring 1.0 in the weakest categories. Those two are the axes here: how specific you get, and how much you carry the conversation forward.

Which means the useful reading is not which of the four styles you land on. It is which of the two behaviours you are currently short on, because that is the one that is costing you replies.

No style is the winning one. The study's top category, a specific reference paired with a question, still only averaged 4.4 out of 10, which is the more honest finding: the gap is behavioural, not stylistic.

Questions

Is this a real personality test?

No, and it does not pretend to be. It is a self-assessment of texting habits along two observable axes. There is no psychometric validation behind it and none is claimed.

Is anything sent to a server?

No. The quiz is JavaScript running on your device. No request, no logging, no storage, no email gate.

Can I get a different result?

Yes, and that is the point. The axes measure habits, and habits move. Retaking it after changing one behaviour is a reasonable way to check whether the change stuck.
