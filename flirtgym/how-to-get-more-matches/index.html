Why you're not getting matches

It is almost always the first photo. Here is the order to fix things in, and why boosts don't work.

Last updated 2026-08-20

It is almost always the first photo. Face clearly visible, unobstructed, well lit, alone in frame. Before changing your bio, your prompts, your swiping strategy or your subscription, fix that one image — it decides whether anyone sees anything else. Then build a set of four to six photos in genuinely different contexts.

Why the first photo carries almost everything

Because the decision is made in about a second, on one image, with no other information. Everything else in your profile only gets looked at by people the first photo already interested.

That makes it the highest-leverage thing you own and the most common place profiles fail. A group photo, sunglasses, a distant shot, bad lighting, or a photo where you look like you are enduring something — each one costs you people who would otherwise have swiped.

The order to fix things in

  1. First photo. Face, light, alone, unobstructed.
  2. The set. Four to six photos, different contexts. One full-body. Cut anything you are unsure about.
  3. Prompts and bio. Specific things people can message you about.
  4. How you open. Only matters once the first three are working.
  5. Paid features. Last, and only after the rest.

Why paying rarely fixes it

Boosts multiply visibility, not appeal. Putting a profile that converts poorly in front of five times as many people produces five times as many people not matching with it.

This is the most common expensive mistake in dating apps, and it is easy to make because the metrics move: more views, more impressions, same matches. The profile was the bottleneck the whole time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why am I not getting matches on dating apps?

Almost always photos, and specifically the first one. Before changing anything else, check that your main photo shows your face clearly, unobstructed, well lit, and alone. Bios and prompts affect whether people message you; the first photo decides whether they swipe at all.

How do you get more matches on Tinder?

Fix the first photo, add photos in genuinely different contexts, and cut anything you are not confident about. Swipe more selectively too — indiscriminate swiping degrades how the app shows you to other people on most platforms.

How do you get more matches on Hinge?

Same photo work, plus prompts that give someone a specific thing to send you. Hinge surfaces prompts prominently, so dead prompts cost more there than on Tinder. Commenting on a specific part of someone's profile rather than sending a plain like also raises your response rate.

Does paying for a dating app get you more matches?

It usually gets you more visibility, which is not the same as more matches. Boosts put a weak profile in front of more people, who then do not match with it. Fix the profile first; paid visibility multiplies whatever you already have, including zero.

How many photos should you have?

Four to six. The weakest photo drags harder than the best one lifts, so cut anything you are unsure about rather than padding to a limit.
