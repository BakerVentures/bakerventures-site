Rate and fix your dating profile

Photos as a set, prompts that give people something to say, and honest feedback instead of polite feedback.

Last updated 2026-08-20

A working profile does three things: one clear photo where your face is unobstructed, four to six photos showing genuinely different contexts, and prompts that hand someone a specific thing to message you about. Most weak profiles are fine on photos and fail entirely on the third. FlirtGym's Rate My Profile and Rate My Look give specific feedback rather than a score.

What actually gets judged

In rough order: the first photo, the rest of the photos as a set, then the text. The text almost never rescues weak photos, and good photos are routinely wasted by prompts that give nobody anything to say.

The set matters more than any single image. Six photos taken at the same angle in the same room tell someone one thing about you. Four photos in four contexts tell them four, and give four possible openers.

The prompt test

Read each prompt answer and ask what someone could send you about it. If the answer is nothing, the prompt is decorative and you have wasted one of your few slots.

"Travel, food, and good vibes" gives nobody anything. "I will defend gas station coffee in front of a jury" gives everybody something. Specific and slightly odd beats broadly appealing, because broadly appealing is invisible.

Getting honest feedback

The hard part is that the people around you are motivated to be kind. Friends soften, partners of friends soften more, and you end up with a profile everyone politely approved and nobody would swipe on.

FlirtGym's Rate My Profile and Rate My Look are built to be specific instead of encouraging — what is not working and what to change, rather than a number. It is private, and unlike posting to a public feedback community, strangers do not end up holding your photos.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you get your dating profile rated?

FlirtGym has Rate My Profile and Rate My Look, which give specific feedback on your photos and bio rather than a number. Free alternatives include asking a friend whose taste you trust, or posting to a feedback community — though public posting means strangers keep your photos.

What makes a dating profile work?

A clear main photo where your face is visible and unobstructed, three to six photos that show different contexts rather than the same angle repeatedly, and prompts that give someone a specific thing to message you about. Most weak profiles fail on the third one.

How many photos should you have?

Four to six. Fewer looks thin; more starts working against you, because the weakest photo drags the average more than the best one lifts it. Cut anything you are not confident about.

What are the most common dating profile mistakes?

Group photos first, sunglasses in every shot, no photo showing your full height or build, prompts answered in one word, and jokes about hating the app. The last one is more common than people think and it reads as resentment.

Should you use AI to write your dating profile bio?

To edit, yes. To write from scratch, be careful — AI-written bios tend to converge on the same voice, and that voice is now recognisable to anyone who reads a lot of profiles. Use it to cut and sharpen what you wrote rather than to generate what you did not.
