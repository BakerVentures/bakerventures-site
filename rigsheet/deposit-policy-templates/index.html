Detailing Deposit and No-Show Policy Templates

Use these sample messages to make booking expectations clear before a customer pays. Replace every bracketed field, give the customer a copy, and obtain advice on the rules that apply to your business.

Not legal advice. These are writing examples, not contracts or statements that a fee is lawful or enforceable. Consumer rights, refund requirements and payment processor rules vary. Have your policy reviewed before using it.

1. Deposit credited to the job

Sample wording

To reserve [service] on [date and time], we request a deposit of [amount]. It will be credited toward the agreed total of [total]. The remaining [balance] is due [when]. Please review our cancellation and refund terms below before confirming.

2. Cancellation and refund terms

Sample wording

To cancel, contact [contact method]. For cancellations received by [specific time and time zone], our refund policy is [describe refund amount and timing]. After that time, our policy is [describe what happens, subject to applicable rights]. We will confirm any refund and its expected processing time in writing.

3. Rescheduling and weather

Sample wording

If weather or an access problem prevents the service, contact [contact] before the appointment. Our rescheduling policy is [notice needed, number of changes and deposit treatment]. If we cancel, we will offer [rescheduled appointment or refund terms]. We will confirm the new date and any price change before proceeding.

4. No-show or unavailable vehicle

Sample wording

Please have the vehicle available at [location] by [time]. If we cannot access it, we will contact you using [method] and wait [time]. If the service cannot proceed, our stated policy is [describe next steps, deposit treatment and any separately agreed fee]. Any new booking will be confirmed with you first.

Before you send a policy

  • State the actual amount and deadline, including time zone.
  • Explain whether the deposit is credited, refunded or transferred in each scenario.
  • Explain what happens when your business cancels.
  • Remove placeholders and avoid promising a universal “non-refundable” rule.
  • Keep the version the customer saw and their confirmation.
