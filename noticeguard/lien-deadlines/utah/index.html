Each deadline, with the statute

Preliminary notice

Filed with the State Construction Registry, counted from the day you commenced providing construction work.

What it counts from. 20 days after first furnishing (commenced work).

Applies to. Original contractor, Subcontractor, Supplier.

Utah Code § 38-1a-501 (1)(a)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-08

“A person who desires to claim a construction lien on real property shall file a preliminary notice with the registry no later than 20 days after the day on which the person commences providing construction work on the real property.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). § 38-1a-501(1)(e): "A person who fails to file a preliminary notice as required in this section may not claim a construction lien."

Also stated next to this deadline.

  • § 38-1a-501(1)(c): a late preliminary notice may still be filed, but the claimant "may not claim a construction lien for construction work the person provides to the construction project before the date that is five days after the preliminary notice is filed."
  • § 38-1a-501(1)(d): a preliminary notice "has no effect if it is filed more than 10 days after the filing of a notice of completion under Section 38-1a-507."
  • § 38-1a-501(3)(b): if work is provided under more than one original contract, the notice requirement must be met for each original contract.

Record notice of construction lien (no notice of completion filed)

Recorded with each applicable county recorder, counted from final completion of the original contract.

What it counts from. 180 days after final completion of the original contract.

Applies to. Original contractor, Subcontractor, Supplier (when no date is entered for notice of completion filed and you do not tick "I provided substantial work after the certificate of occupancy or final inspection").

Utah Code § 38-1a-502 (1)(a)(i)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-08

“A person who desires to claim a construction lien shall submit for recording in the office of each applicable county recorder a notice of construction lien no later than, except as provided in Subsection (1)(b): (i) 180 days after the date on which final completion of the original contract occurs, if no notice of completion is filed under Section 38-1a-507.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). The statute states the notice must be recorded no later than this date in order to claim a construction lien.

Also stated next to this deadline.

  • This clock runs from final completion of the original contract, which is not necessarily the day you last furnished labor or materials.
  • If a notice of completion is later filed under § 38-1a-507, the shorter 90-day rule in § 38-1a-502(1)(a)(ii) applies instead.

Record notice of construction lien (notice of completion filed)

Counted from the filing of the notice of completion, and capped by the 180-day outer limit from final completion.

What it counts from. 90 days after notice of completion filed, but no later than 180 days after final completion of the original contract (whichever is earlier).

Applies to. Original contractor, Subcontractor, Supplier (when a date is entered for notice of completion filed and you do not tick "I provided substantial work after the certificate of occupancy or final inspection").

Utah Code § 38-1a-502 (1)(a)(ii)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-08

“90 days after the date on which a notice of completion is filed under Section 38-1a-507, but not later than 180 days after the date on which final completion of the original contract occurs.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). The statute states the notice must be recorded no later than this date in order to claim a construction lien.

Also stated next to this deadline.

  • The 180-day outer limit applies even when the 90-day count would run longer.

Record notice of construction lien (substantial work after certificate of occupancy)

Counted from final completion of that subcontractor’s own work rather than the original contract.

What it counts from. 180 days after final completion of your own work.

Applies to. Subcontractor (when you tick "I provided substantial work after the certificate of occupancy or final inspection").

Utah Code § 38-1a-502 (1)(b)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-08

“A subcontractor who provides substantial work after a certificate of occupancy is issued or a required final inspection is completed and desires to claim a construction lien shall submit for recording in the office of each applicable county recorder a notice of construction lien no later than 180 days after final completion of that subcontractor's work.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). The statute states the notice must be recorded no later than this date in order to claim a construction lien.

Also stated next to this deadline.

  • The statute does not define "substantial work" by a dollar or time threshold.

Serve copy of recorded lien on owner

Counted from the day the notice of construction lien was filed.

What it counts from. 30 days after notice of construction lien recorded.

Applies to. Original contractor, Subcontractor, Supplier.

Utah Code § 38-1a-502 (4)(a)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-08

“Within 30 days after filing a notice of construction lien, the claimant shall deliver or mail by certified mail a copy of the notice to the reputed owner or the record owner.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). § 38-1a-502(4)(c): failure "precludes the claimant from an award of costs and attorney fees against the reputed owner or record owner in an action to enforce the construction lien." The statute does not state that the lien itself becomes void.

Also stated next to this deadline.

  • § 38-1a-502(4)(b): if the record owner’s current address is not readily available, the last known address from the county assessment rolls may be used.

File action to enforce the lien

Counted from the day the notice of construction lien was filed.

What it counts from. 180 days after notice of construction lien recorded.

Applies to. Original contractor, Subcontractor, Supplier.

Utah Code § 38-1a-701 (2)(a)(ii)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-08

“In order to enforce a preconstruction lien or construction lien, a claimant shall file an action to enforce the lien: (a) except as provided in Subsection (2)(b), within 180 days after the day on which the claimant files: ... (ii) a notice of construction lien under Section 38-1a-502, for a construction lien.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). § 38-1a-701(4)(a): the lien "is automatically and immediately void if an action to enforce the lien is not filed within the time required by this section."

Also stated next to this deadline.

  • § 38-1a-701(3)(a)(i): within the same period the claimant must also record a notice of pendency of the action with each applicable county recorder, or the lien is void except as to parties with actual knowledge.
  • § 38-1a-701(2)(b): if the owner files for bankruptcy protection before the 180 days expire, the period becomes 90 days after the automatic stay is lifted or expires.

Notice of preconstruction service

Filed with the registry, counted from the day preconstruction service commenced.

What it counts from. 20 days after preconstruction service commenced.

Applies to. Preconstruction service.

Utah Code § 38-1a-401 (1)(a)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-08

“A person that desires to claim a preconstruction lien on real property shall file a notice of preconstruction service with the registry no later than 20 days after the person commences providing preconstruction service for the anticipated improvement on the real property.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). § 38-1a-401(1)(b): "A person that fails to file a timely notice of preconstruction service as required in this section may not claim a valid preconstruction lien."

Record notice of preconstruction lien

Counted from completion of the preconstruction service you were not paid for.

What it counts from. 90 days after preconstruction service completed.

Applies to. Preconstruction service.

Utah Code § 38-1a-402 (1)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-08

“Within 90 days after completing a preconstruction service for which a claimant is not paid in full, a claimant who desires to claim a preconstruction lien shall submit for recording with each applicable county recorder a notice of preconstruction lien.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). § 38-1a-402(2): "A claimant who fails to submit a notice of preconstruction lien as provided in Subsection (1) may not claim a preconstruction lien."

Weekend and holiday rollover

NoticeGuard applies this rule to Utah deadlines that land on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday. It is the rule as the app stores it, shown with its source.

Utah R. Civ. P. 6 (a)(1)(C)
Official source · retrieved 2026-09-10

“include the last day of the period, but if the last day is a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the period continues to run until the end of the next day that is not a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday.”

What this rule set does not cover

  • Weekend/holiday rollover follows Utah R. Civ. P. 6(a)(1)(C) and the fixed legal-holiday list in 6(a)(6)(A)-(L). Not modeled: 6(a)(6)(M), "any day designated by the Governor or Legislature as a legal holiday" - an ad hoc designation this app cannot know about in advance - and 6(a)(3), the separate extension when the recording office itself is physically inaccessible (e.g. an emergency closure), which is a real-world event, not a calendar rule.
  • Utah distinguishes final completion of the original contract from the day you last furnished. They are frequently different dates, and § 38-1a-502 runs from final completion, not from your last day on site.
  • Owner-occupied residential projects carry additional restrictions under Title 38, Chapter 11 that are not modeled here.
  • Government and bonded projects have separate notice schemes that are not modeled here.
  • Only the sections quoted below have been read. Related sections (38-1a-102 definitions, 38-1a-503 through 506, 38-1a-601) have not been incorporated.
  • This app generates documents for the preliminary notice, the notice of preconstruction service, and the notice/claim of construction lien - not for serving the recorded lien on the owner (§ 38-1a-502(4)(a) requires delivery or certified mail of the already-generated lien notice itself, not a separate document) or for the action to enforce the lien, which is a civil complaint governed by court rules and local practice, not a form or content list the statute itself prescribes.
