Dating app opening messages that get replies

Specific, short, easy to answer. The four opener patterns that work and the five that quietly don't.

Last updated 2026-08-20

A good opening message is one that could only have been sent to that person. Reference something specific from her profile, ask something answerable in a sentence, and keep it under about fifteen words. Openers that would work on anyone get replies at the rate of a message sent to anyone.

What separates an opener that works from one that doesn't?

Three things, in order of how much they matter.

Specificity. Every generic opener she receives makes a specific one stand out more. A detail from her third photo is worth more than any clever line, because it proves you looked.

Low reply cost. She should be able to answer in a sentence without thinking hard. Open-ended questions feel generous and land as homework.

Brevity. Under fifteen words. A long first message asks for investment before any has been earned, and reads as effort in the wrong direction.

Openers that reliably work

  • The specific detail. Pick the oddest thing in her profile and ask about it. Odd beats attractive here — she has answered questions about the attractive parts before.
  • The playful mislabel. Deliberately get a small detail wrong in a way that invites correction. Correcting someone is one of the easiest replies there is.
  • The direct opinion. Take a light position on something in her profile. Position invites response; neutrality invites nothing.
  • The honest compliment plus a hook. A compliment alone terminates. A compliment with a question attached does not.

Openers to skip

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good opening message on a dating app?

One that could only have been sent to that person. Reference something specific from her profile, ask something she can answer in a sentence, and keep it under about fifteen words. Openers that would work on anyone get the reply rate of a message sent to anyone.

Does 'hey' ever work?

Occasionally, and almost entirely on the strength of your photos rather than the message. Treat that as the ceiling: 'hey' delegates the entire conversation to her, and the people it works on are people who would have replied to anything.

Should you use pickup lines?

Rarely worth it. A line that is obviously copied signals that you sent it to twenty people, which is the exact impression a first message should avoid. Humour works; canned humour usually does not.

How long should a first message be?

Short. One or two sentences, roughly under fifteen words. A long first message asks for a large investment before any has been earned, and the most common result is no reply at all.

Should you ask a question in the first message?

Usually yes, because a question makes replying easy. The exception is a question so generic it is work to answer. 'What's your favourite thing to do on weekends?' is a job interview; 'okay but is the dog yours or are you borrowing it for the photo' is a conversation.
