What the flow tells you

Discharge in cubic feet per second describes the volume passing this gauge. Gauge height is the water level relative to the station datum; it is not the depth where you will step. Compare the recent trend at this same station rather than comparing its height to another river.

A rising trace is a reason to reassess your plan. A steady or low reading does not establish safe wading: current, footing, visibility, weather and your exit route still matter. No universal flow threshold makes this reach safe.

Hatch notes and patterns to consider

Tailwater planning: start with small midge and mayfly imitations; observe actual insects before choosing a fly.

General planning examples, not a river-specific hatch report
Observed insect Pattern to compare
Small midges Zebra Midge or Griffith’s Gnat
