Each deadline, with the statute

File lien statement (contractor)

Filed with the county clerk, counted from this claimant's own last day of work performed or materials furnished - but no later than 150 days after the project's substantial completion, if that comes sooner.

What it counts from. 150 days after last furnishing (last day on site), but no later than 150 days after final completion of the original contract (whichever is earlier).

Applies to. Original contractor.

Wyo. Stat. § 29-2-106 (a)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-21

“Except as provided in subsection (c) of this section, any contractor asserting a lien under this chapter shall file his lien statement within one hundred fifty (150) days ... of the earlier of: (A) After the last day when work was performed or materials furnished under contract; (B) From the date of substantial completion of the project on which work was performed or materials were furnished under contract.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). The statute's own operative language: the contractor "shall file his lien statement within" this period.

File lien statement (subcontractor, supplier, or preconstruction service provider)

Filed with the county clerk, counted from this claimant's own last day of work performed or materials furnished - but no later than 120 days after the project's substantial completion, if that comes sooner.

What it counts from. 120 days after last furnishing (last day on site), but no later than 120 days after final completion of the original contract (whichever is earlier).

Applies to. Subcontractor, Supplier, Preconstruction service.

Wyo. Stat. § 29-2-106 (a)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-21

“Except as provided in subsection (c) of this section, any contractor asserting a lien under this chapter shall file his lien statement within one hundred fifty (150) days and every other person asserting a lien under this chapter shall file within one hundred twenty (120) days: (i) Of the earlier of: (A) After the last day when work was performed or materials furnished under contract; (B) From the date of substantial completion of the project on which work was performed or materials were furnished under contract; or (ii) With respect to a subcontractor, after the last day he performed work at the direction of the contractor or other person authorized to provide direction.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). The statute's own operative language: every other claimant "shall file within" this period.

Also stated next to this deadline.

  • No contract between the owner and the contractor can restrict a subcontractor's or materialman's own right to file a lien (§ 29-2-106(b)).

Commence action to foreclose or enforce the lien

Counted from the date the lien statement was filed. The lien stops existing after this period unless an action has been commenced.

What it counts from. 180 days after notice of construction lien recorded.

Applies to. Original contractor, Subcontractor, Supplier, Preconstruction service.

Wyo. Stat. § 29-2-109 (sole operative sentence)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-21

“All actions to foreclose or enforce a lien under this chapter shall be commenced within one hundred eighty (180) days after the filing of the lien statement. No lien shall continue to exist except by virtue of the provisions of this chapter for more than one hundred eighty (180) days after the lien is filed unless an action to foreclose the lien is instituted.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). The statute's own mechanism: "No lien shall continue to exist... for more than one hundred eighty (180) days after the lien is filed unless an action to foreclose the lien is instituted."

Weekend and holiday rollover

NoticeGuard does not model a weekend or holiday extension for Wyoming. The dates above are the plain count from the date you enter. Check whether a Saturday, Sunday or holiday changes your date.

What this rule set does not cover

  • The notice of intention to file a lien (§ 29-2-107) is NOT modeled - it runs backward ("sent no later than twenty (20) days prior to filing a lien statement"), not forward from a fixed anchor this app's engine can express as a due date. The same discipline this app already applies to Colorado's backward-counted notice of intent.
  • The record owner's optional notice of substantial completion (§ 29-2-106(c)-(d)) does not change either filing deadline's length - it only creates a rebuttable presumption of the substantial-completion date itself. This app treats the substantial-completion date you enter as that date directly, whether or not a notice was recorded.
  • The agreement-to-extend-filing-period mechanism (§ 29-2-106(e), up to double the normal period) and the separate cooperative-utility lien (§ 29-2-106(f), 180/360 days) are not modeled - data-dependent and a narrow claimant category, respectively.
  • This app could not confirm, in this pass, whether Wyoming's lien statement has a statutory content-element list of its own (distinct from the notice-of-intention content referenced in § 29-2-107(b)) - no document is generated for this state until that is verified, rather than guessing at required content.
  • Weekend/holiday rollover is NOT modeled - no general Wyoming computation-of-time statute was researched this pass.
  • Sourcing gap, stated plainly: the statute text quoted here was read from Justia's Wyoming Statutes mirror - a commercial legal publisher - and not from the State of Wyoming's own site. Justia's mirror is reputable and labels itself current to 2025, but it is not the primary source and has not been re-verified word-for-word against the state's own page. Named for the same reason Arkansas's, Georgia's, New Mexico's and Tennessee's equivalent gaps are named.
  • Only §§ 29-2-106, 29-2-107, and 29-2-109 have been read closely. The rest of Chapter 2 - including the leasehold and tenant-improvement lien variants (§§ 29-2-104, 29-2-105) - was reviewed but not otherwise incorporated.

About the NoticeGuard app
