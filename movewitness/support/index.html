What the record says, and what it doesn’t

MoveWitness turns a walkthrough into room-by-room photos and notes and a dated PDF you can hand to a landlord, a tenant or a mediator. Most support questions are really one question: how much does that report prove? This page answers that first.

Not legal advice. MoveWitness is a documentation tool, not a law firm, and nothing on this page or in the app is legal advice. Landlord-tenant and deposit rules vary a lot by state and city. For a contested deposit or a court filing, consult a qualified attorney or your local housing authority. The app’s job is to make sure you have a clear, dated record to bring them.

The three words a report can print under a photo

This is the part of the app that is easiest to get quietly wrong, so it is worth reading before anything else. A photo file either carries its own capture date or it does not — a screenshot, an edited copy, or anything a share sheet or messaging app re-encoded usually does not. MoveWitness never guesses the difference. It prints one of exactly three words, and the report carries this legend itself so the caveat travels with the file.

Photo-time labels, as printed in the report
LabelWhat it meansWhat it does not mean
Captured The photo file itself carried a capture date, and that is the date shown. Not a certified or verified date — it is the date the file claims.
Imported The file carried no capture date, so the time shown is when you added it to the app. Never presented as the day the photo was taken.
Recorded The row was written before the app distinguished the two, so it is one or the other and the app says it does not know which. Not a fallback guess — it is an explicit “capture or import time, not recorded which”.

No capture date is ever inferred, defaulted or filled in. All three wordings come from one place in the code, and every surface that shows a time — both PDFs, both CSVs, the room screen and its accessibility label — goes through it.

What a MoveWitness report establishes

It does

  • Keep each room’s photos, captions and condition notes together, in one record.
  • State, per photo, which kind of time it carries.
  • Lock the inspection when you complete it, so the report cannot quietly reflect later edits made through the app.
  • Name the suggested rooms that have no matching entry, on single-inspection exports, instead of implying completeness.
  • Print a SHA-256 content digest on the PDF, so an altered underlying record produces a different hash.

It does not

  • Prove when a photo was taken, or when the report was created.
  • Carry a signature. The digest is a content digest and is labelled as one.
  • Decide a deposit dispute, or recover money.
  • Give legal advice, or tell you what your landlord may withhold.
  • Put a digest on the CSV exports — neither CSV carries one.

Who it is for, and what it is not

Who is MoveWitness for?
Both tenants and landlords documenting a rental’s condition at move-in and move-out to prevent or resolve security-deposit disputes.
Is this legal advice, or will it win my dispute?
No, and it can’t promise that. It produces an organized record of your walkthrough. Whether that record wins is up to your landlord, mediator, or court, and your local law. For anything you’re relying on in a dispute, check with a qualified attorney or your local housing authority.
Does MoveWitness prove when my photos were taken?
No. MoveWitness does not prove when a photo was taken, and nothing in the app should be read as a certified date. What the report does show, for every photo, is which kind of time it carries: “Captured” where the photo file has its own capture date, “Imported” where it has none and the time is when you added it, and “Recorded” for photos added before the app told the two apart. A legend in the report defines all three, so a reader cannot mistake one for the other. It also keeps each room’s photos and notes together, locks the inspection when you finish, and produces a PDF report you can share. How any particular landlord or court weighs that record is a legal question for an attorney or your housing authority.
Does it tell me my state’s deposit deadline?
For eleven states it shows a cited summary of the security-deposit statute on the property screen — Utah, California, Texas, Florida, New York, Illinois, Pennsylvania, Ohio, Georgia, North Carolina and Washington. Each entry carries its citation, the official source URL and the date it was last checked. It is a summary for orientation, not advice, and for the other states the app says nothing rather than generalising.

The walkthrough and the lock

How does the side-by-side comparison work?
Label rooms at move-in; at move-out, shoot the same rooms and MoveWitness pairs them in the report so a reviewer sees before and after together.
What exactly happens when I complete an inspection?
It locks. Every write the app issues carries its own “not yet completed” condition, evaluated atomically, so a stale screen cannot slip an edit past it and no room, note or photo can be added, changed or removed afterwards through the app. The honest limit: that lock binds the app, not the database file itself, so the app’s own wording is “through the app” rather than a claim that the file is immutable.
How many rooms or properties can I add?
As many as you need. MoveWitness is free right now, with no room or property limit.
Do both the tenant and landlord need the app?
No. One person documents; the exported PDF can be shared with anyone.

Exports, and the digest

How do I share the report with my landlord?
Once your walkthrough is done, use Export to generate the PDF, then the share sheet lets you email it, message it, or save it to Files or Drive — it’s a normal PDF you fully control. We’d suggest saving a copy somewhere off the phone too, so you always have the dated original.
Can I add signatures?
Not yet. MoveWitness doesn’t have a signature feature or a cover page. Every exported PDF report carries a SHA-256 content digest instead — a fingerprint of the room facts, the photo bytes and your captions that went into it, computed before the PDF is drawn. It is not a signature, it does not prove when the file was created, and it is not a check on the PDF file itself, so editing the PDF afterwards will not change the digest printed inside it.
I regenerated the same report and got a different digest. Is that a bug?
No. The time the file was generated is part of what the digest covers, so regenerating an untouched inspection gives a different digest. That means it fingerprints one generated file rather than being a check you can re-run later to test an inspection — which is exactly what the app’s own terms screen says, and worth knowing before you describe it to anyone else.
Do the CSV exports carry a digest too?
No. Neither CSV carries a report-level digest. Each has a per-photo SHA-256 column, which is a different thing: it fingerprints individual photo files, not the report.

Where everything is stored

Where are my photos and notes stored?
On your device. MoveWitness has no account and no studio server, so your photos, notes, and reports live only on your phone — we never see them and nothing is uploaded to us. They’re included if your phone backs up to iCloud or Google, and they’re gone if you delete the app, so export or save any PDF you want to keep.
What if I lose my phone?
Because storage is local, export your report as soon as an inspection is done. Deleting the app deletes the photos.

The privacy policy sets out the backup question in full, including why the records were deliberately left in your own iCloud backup rather than held back from it.

Bugs and requests

Something’s broken. How do I report it?
Email us and include: (1) device and OS version, (2) what you were doing (adding a room, taking a photo, exporting the PDF…), (3) what you expected vs. what happened, and (4) a screenshot. That usually points us right at it.
Can I request a feature?
Please do. Tell us what you’d want and why — real move-day feedback is exactly what we want. We can’t promise timing, but every request gets read and logged.
