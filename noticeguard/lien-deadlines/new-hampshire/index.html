Each deadline, with the statute

Secure the lien by attachment

The lien exists automatically once labor or materials are furnished, but continues for only 120 days after this claimant's own last furnishing - and must be secured within that window by a court-ordered attachment of the property, or it lapses.

What it counts from. 120 days after last furnishing (last day on site).

Applies to. Original contractor, Subcontractor, Supplier, Preconstruction service.

N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 447:9, § 447:10 (sole operative sentences)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-21

“447:9 Duration. – The lien created by RSA 447:2-7, inclusive, shall continue for 120 days after the services are performed, or the materials, supplies or other things are furnished, unless payment therefor is previously made, and shall take precedence of all prior claims except liens on account of taxes. 447:10 How Secured. – Any such lien may be secured by attachment of the property upon which it exists at any time while the lien continues, the writ and return thereon distinctly expressing that purpose.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). The statute's own mechanism: the lien "shall continue for 120 days" and no longer - an attachment obtained after that window secures nothing, since the lien it would attach to no longer exists.

Also stated next to this deadline.

  • Unlike every other state in this app, there is no recording or filing step that creates or preserves this lien - only a court-ordered attachment of the property does, and it must happen inside the same 120 days.

What this rule set does not cover

  • No document is generated for New Hampshire - confirmed absent, not merely unresearched. The chapter has no recordable lien instrument at all; the lien exists automatically once labor/materials are furnished, and is preserved only by a court writ of attachment (§ 447:10), whose content ("distinctly expressing that purpose") is a court-filing mechanic, not a claimant-drafted document this app can template.
  • The subcontractor notice-of-intent-to-claim-a-lien (§ 447:5) is NOT modeled - its own text requires giving it BEFORE performing the labor or furnishing the material, and § 447:6 separately allows giving it afterward instead, with no day-count cutoff either way (late notice only limits the lien to amounts still owed when given). Neither shape is a forward due date.
  • The recurring 30-day account-furnishing duty for anyone who gave notice under §§ 447:5-7 (§ 447:8: must furnish a written account of labor/materials at least once every 30 days) is not modeled - a repeating obligation, not a single due date this app's engine expresses.
  • Public-works liens against state/municipal contracts (§§ 447:15-18, a bond-claim scheme with its own 90-day filing and one-year petition deadlines) are not modeled - this app covers private construction only.
  • Narrow categories outside ordinary building construction (vessels, brick-making, lumber/log driving, railroads - §§ 447:1, 447:3, 447:4, 447:7) are not modeled.
  • Weekend/holiday rollover is NOT modeled - no general New Hampshire computation-of-time statute was researched this pass.
  • Only §§ 447:2, 447:5, 447:6, 447:9, and 447:10 have been read. The rest of Chapter 447 - including attachment priority (§§ 447:11, 447:12, 447:12-a, 447:12-b) - has not been incorporated.
