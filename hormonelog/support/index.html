No account, and what follows from it

Do I need an account to use HormoneLog?
No. There is no sign-up and no sign-in, and the app works fully offline. Because there is no account, there is also nothing to recover — see the question about losing your phone below.
Where is my data stored?
In a database on your own device. The free text most likely to be sensitive — your daily notes and the dose text you type — is additionally encrypted at rest with a key generated on your phone and held in its secure hardware store (the iOS Keychain or Android Keystore). That key is never transmitted anywhere, including to us.
If I lose my phone, can you recover my log?
No, and it is important that you know this before you rely on the app. On iPhone the database is deliberately kept out of your device backups and the encryption key never leaves the handset, so if the phone is lost, wiped or replaced there is no copy anywhere and no key escrow — nobody, including us, can get it back. Export your data from Settings whenever it would hurt to lose it.
How do I move my data to a new phone?
On the old device, Settings → Export as JSON, then open that file and copy all of its text. On the new device, Settings → Restore from backup and paste it in. It is a paste, not a file picker, and it replaces everything currently on the new device — which cannot be undone.
Is there cloud backup or sync?
Not yet. The toggle in Settings saves your preference only; as the app says when you turn it on, cloud sync ships in a future update and your data stays local-only until then.
Can I get my data out?
Yes, on any plan. Settings offers a full export as JSON and a CSV of your symptom log, both handed to your phone’s share sheet so you choose where they go.
How do I delete everything?
Settings → Delete everything. It permanently removes all your local data — logs, regimen and reports — immediately, and it cannot be undone.

What can I actually log?
36 symptoms grouped into seven categories, each on a 0–3 severity scale; mood, energy, sleep quality and stress on 1–5 sliders; whether you drank or exercised; your period and its flow; a free-text note for the day; and an HRT regimen with its doses, reminders and adherence. If you have set your stage to post-menopausal, the period section is hidden.
Do I have to log all 36 every day?
No, and the index is built so that you are not penalised for tracking more. Only days you actually logged count, and the score is an average rescaled rather than a total, so adding a symptom to your list does not push your number up by itself.

The Symptom Burden Index

What is the Symptom Burden Index?
It is HormoneLog’s own summary of what you logged, on a 0–100 scale where lower is better, with bands labelled Minimal, Mild, Moderate and High. It is an average of your 0–3 ratings rescaled — not a total — so tracking more symptoms doesn’t make your score look worse, and only days you actually logged count towards it. It is a summary of your own entries, not a clinical measurement and not a diagnosis.
Is it a validated clinical score?
No. It is self-authored, built on HormoneLog’s own symptom taxonomy, and it is never described as validated or clinically proven, because it is neither. It summarises your own logs so a pattern is easier to see and easier to show someone. Read on its own, out of context, it can be incomplete or misleading.

HRT, doses and reminders

Will it tell me what to take, or change my dose?
No, never. The app’s own line is “Tracking tool, not medical advice. Discuss changes with your prescriber.” It records the dose you type in and reminds you to take it; it does not calculate, suggest or adjust anything.
Will a notification show my symptoms or medication on my lock screen?
No. The reminder text is fixed and deliberately says nothing specific — “Evening check-in / A minute to log how today went” and “Dose reminder / Tap to log today’s dose”. No notification ever names a symptom or a medication.
What does adherence actually measure?
Doses you marked as taken against doses that were scheduled. A dose logged late still counts as taken, because the point is whether you took it rather than whether you tapped on time.

Apple Health

What does it do with Apple Health?
On iPhone, and only if you grant permission, it reads four things — sleep analysis, heart rate variability, resting heart rate and body temperature — so it can show them alongside your symptom log. It never writes anything back to Apple Health, and that data never leaves your device. You can refuse or revoke the permission in iOS Settings at any time and the rest of the app works normally. Android has no equivalent integration, so those options are hidden there.

The report you take to your prescriber

What is actually sent when I generate a doctor’s report?
Only the summary that is already on the screen: the index and its category breakdown, how often each symptom appeared, and a short text summary of your regimen. That goes over an encrypted connection to a rendering service that lays it out as a PDF and keeps nothing afterwards. Your raw daily entries, your mood, energy and sleep ratings, and your free-text notes are never sent.

How do I restore a purchase?
Settings → Subscription → Restore purchases, or the Restore purchases link on the plan screen, signed into the same Apple ID or Google account you bought with.
How do I cancel, or ask a billing question?
Settings → Manage subscription opens your App Store or Google Play subscription settings, which is where billing is handled — we don’t process payments. Cancelling stops the next renewal and you keep access until the period you’ve paid for ends.
How do I get a refund?
Refunds are handled by Apple or Google under their own policies, not by us. Use reportaproblem.apple.com on iOS, or play.google.com/store/account on Android. If the store gives you trouble, tell us and we’ll help where we can.
