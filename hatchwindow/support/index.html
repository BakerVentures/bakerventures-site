Where the numbers come from, what they are worth, and what the app will not pretend to know.

Before you go

Conditions and feeding windows are estimates built from public data plus solunar math and a curated hatch list. They are a starting point, not a promise — rivers and fish don’t read the forecast. Always check current local flow and weather yourself, follow your state’s fishing regulations and access rules, and put safety first on the water. Hatch Window doesn’t track your license or local rules; confirm them with your state agency before you fish.

The water numbers

Where do the water conditions come from?
The USGS public water data service — real, public streamflow and gauge-height data. It’s attributed in the app. We never show a made-up number.
Why does a spot say “no current data”?
That gauge isn’t reporting right now, or the network’s down. Rather than guess, Hatch Window tells you the reading is unavailable.
There’s no data for my river or spot.
That usually means there’s no nearby public streamflow gauge for that water — Hatch Window leans on gauges that exist, so ungauged creeks come up thin. The solunar feeding windows still work anywhere, since they’re calculated from date and location. Send us the spot and we’ll see whether there’s a usable nearby gauge we can wire in.
How current is a reading, and can it change afterwards?
USGS data is provisional and subject to revision, which is the agency’s own characterisation of it rather than a hedge of ours. A reading can be corrected later, so treat a number from any gauge as the best current estimate rather than a measurement of record.
Can I add my own spots?
Yes. Add any spot; if you know its USGS gauge number, Hatch Window will pull live conditions for it.

Timing: sun, solunar, and the week ahead

How are the “best times” calculated?
On your device, from sunrise/sunset, twilight, and solunar major/minor feeding periods for the spot’s location — standard astronomy math, no server.
Is solunar theory actually proven?
No, and the app says so in as many words. Solunar timing is a traditional angling heuristic, not a scientifically validated model. It is in the app because a lot of anglers plan by it and because it costs nothing to compute; it is not presented as a prediction of whether fish will eat.
What does the 7-day outlook rank?
Each of the next seven days by its best solunar window for that spot, so you can pick a day rather than only checking today. It ranks the window, not the fishing — weather and flow still decide the day.
Why does the dashboard put one spot above another?
The multi-spot dashboard orders your saved spots by a documented “fishing well right now” heuristic that favours fresh, steady flow. It is a sort order built from the readings the app already has; no per-river score is invented to produce it.

Flies, and what the hatch chart covers

Are the fly recommendations reliable?
They come from a curated database of 24 hatches matched to real, commonly-tied patterns, filtered by month, water type and region. It covers four regions — Mountain West, Northeast (Catskills/Appalachians), Midwest (Driftless/Great Lakes) and South (Southern Appalachians/Southeast tailwaters) — and three water types: tailwater, freestone and spring creek. There are no saltwater or steelhead charts. Timing shifts with the year, elevation and water temperature, so treat the list as a starting point, not a promise.

Why are all the starter spots out West?
Because that is where the curated spot list was built first. The Northeast, Midwest and South hatch charts are complete and in the app now; the starter waters that ship with it are Mountain West. Adding your own spot anywhere gets you the right regional chart alongside it.

Your spots, your log, and going offline

Does the app work without signal on the river?
Your saved spots, catch log, feeding windows and hatch list work offline. Live streamflow and weather need a connection, so grab a look before you lose signal and you’ll have the latest reading with you.
Where is my catch log stored?
On your device, in a local database. Hatch Window has no account and no server of ours, so your catches, notes and spot names never come to us. One thing does leave your phone: when you open a spot, its coordinates or its USGS gauge number go to USGS and Open-Meteo to fetch that spot’s conditions. Nothing else goes with them. Your log is in your phone’s iCloud backup if that is on, and deleting the app deletes the copy on the device. There is no full export of the log yet — you can share one outing at a time as text — so treat the phone and your backup as the only copies you have.
Can I share what worked?
Yes — generate a text summary of an outing (spot, date, conditions, flies that worked) to share.
Does the app remember what worked at a spot?
Yes. Entries you log feed a “what worked here before” view on that spot, which is the main reason to keep the log at all rather than just reading conditions.

Licences, regulations and what this app is not

Do I need a fishing license?
Almost certainly, and regulations vary by water. Hatch Window doesn’t track your license or local rules — confirm them with your state agency before you fish, and put safety first on the water.

Cost

Free, and unlocked. There is no subscription, no in-app purchase, no paywall and no advertising, so there is nothing to buy, cancel or refund.

If that ever changes, this page will describe the real cancellation, refund and restore steps before the change ships.

Do I need to pay for anything?
No. Hatch Window is free and every feature ships unlocked — there’s no subscription, no in-app purchase, no paywall, and no ads. Nothing in this version of the app can charge you.

Reporting a problem

Something’s broken, or a reading looks wrong. How do I report it?
Email us with: (1) device and OS version, (2) what you were doing (viewing conditions, logging a catch…), (3) the location if it’s data-related, (4) expected vs. actual, and (5) a screenshot. If a specific reading looked off, tell us the location and we’ll check our data source.
