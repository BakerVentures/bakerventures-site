Why the date of each mile matters

The rate is not a property of the tax year. It is a property of the day the mile was driven. A 100-mile delivery day on June 29 is worth $72.50 and the identical day on July 1 is worth $76.00. There is no averaging, no weighting by when you filed, and no option to pick the better one.

So a mid-year rate change turns a single annual mileage figure into a number that cannot be right. Run the whole year at 72.5 cents and every second-half mile is underclaimed by 3.5 cents. Run it at 76 cents and every first-half mile is overclaimed by the same amount. On 10,000 miles either error is $350.

This is only the second mid-year change in roughly a decade, which is exactly why so much software and so many spreadsheets still assume one rate per year. If you are using a tool that asked you for a year and a mileage total and nothing else, it is giving you the wrong number for 2026.

What this does with a date it does not recognise

It excludes the line and tells you, rather than pricing it at a guessed rate. A 2025 or 2027 date is not a rounding problem, it is a different rate table, and silently folding it into a 2026 total is how a wrong figure ends up on a return looking confident. Unparseable lines and negative mileage are reported the same way.

Keeping a log that holds up

A mileage total on its own is not a record. The IRS asks for the mileage for each use, the date, the destination and the business purpose, kept at or near the time of the trip. A figure reconstructed in April from memory is the weakest version of this and a contemporaneous log is the strongest.

Questions

Is this tax advice?

No. It multiplies miles by the published rate for the period each mile falls in. It does not tell you whether you qualify to use the standard mileage method, whether actual expenses would serve you better, or what you owe. Ask a tax professional before you file.

Does my log get uploaded?

No. The parsing and the arithmetic are JavaScript running on your device. There is no request, no logging and no storage. You can disconnect and it still works.

Can I use the standard mileage rate and also deduct gas?

No. The standard mileage rate exists instead of itemising your vehicle operating costs, so claiming both for the same miles is double-counting. Which method you are eligible for and which is better for you is a question for a tax professional, not a calculator.

What about parking and tolls?

Those are separate from the per-mile figure rather than inside it, so this calculator does not include them and you should not expect the number here to cover them.

Why does it refuse a 2027 date?

Because the 2027 rates are a different table and this page only holds 2026. Pricing a 2027 mile at a 2026 rate to avoid showing you an error would be the wrong answer delivered quietly, which is worse than an error.
