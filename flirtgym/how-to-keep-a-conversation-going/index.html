How to keep a conversation going

Conversations die when one person does all the work. Here is what actually keeps one alive.

Last updated 2026-08-20

Give her something to react to in every message. Conversations die when one person carries all the work of continuing them. That means statements with a hook instead of bare questions, specifics instead of generalities, and offering something of yourself rather than interviewing her. Match her message length, and move to a date sooner than feels comfortable.

Why conversations fizzle

Almost always the same failure: the exchange became an interview. Question, answer, unrelated question. It feels polite and it reads as an interrogation, because nothing accumulates and nothing gets referenced.

The tell is whether either of you could drop out of the conversation without it changing. If your messages could be sent to anyone with her profile, there is no conversation happening — just two people taking turns.

What keeps one alive

  • Statements with a hook. "You are clearly the person who plans the whole trip" invites a response without demanding one.
  • Callbacks. Referencing something from twenty messages ago is the cheapest way to signal that this is a specific conversation with a specific person.
  • Small disagreement. Mild, playful, low stakes. Agreement ends threads; a difference of opinion gives one somewhere to go.
  • Something of yourself. Questions alone put all the exposure on her. Offering first makes it an exchange.

The interview test

Interview versionConversation version
"What do you do for work?""You do not strike me as someone who ended up in accounting on purpose"
"Do you like to travel?""The Lisbon photo is doing a lot of work in this profile"
"How was your weekend?""Tell me the hike was better than the last one you complained about"
"What are you into?""Guessing you have strong opinions about at least one of these"

Every version on the right does the same two things: it is specific to her, and it can be answered in four words or forty.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you keep a conversation going on a dating app?

Give her something to react to in every message. That means statements with a hook rather than bare questions, specifics rather than generalities, and a bit of yourself rather than pure interviewing. A conversation dies when one side has to do all the work of continuing it.

Why do my conversations always fizzle out?

Usually because they turned into an interview. Question, answer, new unrelated question is a pattern that feels polite and reads as an interrogation. Nothing accumulates, nothing gets referenced, and there is no reason for either person to keep going.

What should you talk about?

Whatever produces a reaction rather than a report. Opinions, small disagreements, shared references, and specifics from her profile all work. 'What do you do for work?' produces a report. 'You do not strike me as someone who ended up in accounting on purpose' produces a conversation.

How do you move from texting to a date?

Sooner than feels comfortable, and with something specific. A concrete suggestion is easier to say yes to than an open invitation, and texting has a shelf life — the longer a conversation stays in the app, the more likely it quietly ends there.

How long should messages be?

Roughly match her length, and cap it well short of a paragraph. Sending twice what she sends reads as more invested than she is; sending a third of it reads as disinterest.
