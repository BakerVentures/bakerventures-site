Last updated: September 2, 2026

Nearvo (“we”, “us”, “the app”) is a couples app for daily connection, communication and conflict skills, and sexual intimacy, operated by Baker Ventures LLC. This policy explains what we collect, why, and how you can control it. It’s written to match exactly what the app does — not a generic template.

Nearvo is for adults. You must be 18 or older to create an account. We never store profile data for anyone who does not pass the age check.

Relationship & onboarding data

Your answers to onboarding questions drive your personalized plan and content. One of these questions concerns comfort with sexual intimacy topics. We treat this as sensitive information under data protection law (for example, GDPR Article 9): we ask for your explicit, separately-withdrawable consent before using it, we exclude it from analytics entirely, and it is included in the account export and deletion tools described below.

Couple data

When you pair with a partner, we store the connection between your two accounts, your relationship stage/anniversary, and your subscription status. Your account data is only ever visible to you and the partner you’re paired with — never to other users.

What the two of you create together

Everything in this section is visible to you and your partner and nobody else. It is removed when you delete your account, and the per-item notes say what happens if only one of you leaves.

  • Your answers to the daily question — written, or recorded as a voice note if you choose. Voice answers are stored as audio files in a private location and are only ever reachable through short-lived links. Neither of you can read or hear the other’s answer until you have both answered.
  • Comments and reactions on each other’s answers.
  • Photos you add as Memories, with an optional caption and the date the memory is of. We strip photo metadata — including GPS location — on your device, before the picture is uploaded. Photos are stored privately, never at a public address.
  • Important dates — birthdays, anniversaries and dates you name yourself. A birthday is personal data about the person whose birthday it is, so deleting that person’s account deletes it; dates about the two of you stay with whoever remains. Reminders are scheduled on your own device; no date is sent anywhere to schedule one.
  • Love notes — short messages and emoji you send each other. The notification on your partner’s phone never contains the note itself, only who it is from.
  • Mood check-ins — a daily one-to-five rating and an optional note, shown to your partner. This is a self-reported feeling, not a medical or health record, and we never diagnose anything from it. We treat it carefully anyway: it is excluded from analytics entirely, only you can edit or delete your own entries, and the date is set by our server so a history cannot be rewritten.
  • Appreciations — the weekly note about something you appreciated, and the year of them you can read back.
  • Your bucket list and saved date ideas, including whether you have done them.
  • Your progress — streaks, course lessons completed, quiz results and their history, and games you have played together.

The AI coach

If you use the coach, we store your conversation so you can come back to it. A conversation you start privately is private permanently — it cannot later be turned into a shared one, and your partner cannot read it. Your messages are sent to Anthropic to generate a reply; Anthropic does not use them to train models. Coach conversations are excluded from analytics and are deleted with your account.

Keeping the app safe

If you block someone or report content, we store that so we can act on it. When our content filter stops something from being posted, we record which rule matched and how long the text was — never the text itself. Telling you something will not be posted and then keeping it would be worse than having no filter at all.

What we do not collect

  • No precise location — only your timezone, to time your daily prompt
  • No contacts, no photo library access beyond the avatar you choose to upload
  • No advertising identifier and no third-party advertising SDKs — we don’t show ads or track you across other apps, so we never show an App Tracking Transparency prompt
  • No raw daily-question answers from you or your partner are ever sent to our AI coaching provider — only redacted categories and goals. (If you use the coach, what you type to it is sent to generate a reply — see “The AI coach” above.)
  • No health data. Intimacy check-ins are self-reported preferences, not medical or health records.

3. How we protect it

Everything travels over TLS. Nothing in this app is sent over an unencrypted connection.

At rest, your data sits in a managed Postgres database with encrypted storage and encrypted automated backups. Access is governed row by row by database policies rather than by application code: the rule that you can see your own data and your partner’s shared data, and nobody else’s, is enforced by the database itself — so a bug in the app cannot hand somebody another couple’s answers.

Two protections are worth naming, because they cover the two places this app holds something unusually personal:

  • The daily answers. Neither of you can read the other’s answer until you have both answered. That is not a display rule in the app — the database will not return your partner’s answer to you until yours exists, so there is no version of the app, and no request anybody can craft, that reveals it early.
  • Photos and voice notes. Stored privately, never at a public address, and reachable only through short-lived links generated at the moment you view them. Photo metadata, including GPS location, is stripped on your device before upload — so we never receive where a photo was taken.

Sensitive-topic preferences and mood check-ins are excluded from analytics entirely, are never included in coaching context, and can never appear in an image the app produces for sharing.

Our own access to production data is limited to the people who need it to keep the service running and to answer support requests, and it is logged. We do not read your answers, notes or messages, and nothing in this app or our internal tooling is built to let us do so casually.

No system is perfectly secure and we will not claim otherwise. If a breach affects your personal data we will notify you and the relevant supervisory authority as the law requires, without undue delay.

5. Your controls — deletion, export, and withdrawing consent

  • Delete your account from the app’s Profile settings at any time. This permanently removes your account and all associated data.
  • Export your data from Profile → Safety & privacy. You get a machine-readable copy of everything the app holds about you, which is your right under GDPR Article 20. It deliberately excludes your partner’s words: their answers, notes and messages are their personal data, not yours, and an export is not a way to obtain them.
  • Withdraw consent for sensitive-topic content at any time from Profile settings.

6. Data retention

We keep your data for as long as your account is active. This is a diary of a relationship, and one that quietly deleted last year would not be much of a diary.

When you delete your account, deletion means deletion. Your account and everything attached to it is removed from our live systems immediately and permanently. It is not frozen, deactivated, anonymised, or held in case you come back. There is no restore.

Database backups are kept on a rolling schedule and are never retained for more than 30 days. During that window a copy of your data may still exist inside a backup snapshot, because a backup that could be edited after the fact would not be a backup. Those snapshots are never used to repopulate a deleted account and are only ever restored wholesale in a disaster. Within 30 days of your deletion, no copy remains anywhere.

Two things outlive the account, and only these two. Records of a purchase are kept for as long as tax and accounting law requires — a legal obligation we cannot waive on request. And aggregate, non-identifying counts (for example, how many couples finished onboarding in a given month) remain, because they contain nothing about any individual and cannot be traced back to one.

What happens to your partner’s data. Deleting your account does not delete theirs. Your own words go. Things that are theirs stay with them. Anything genuinely about the two of you jointly stays with whoever remains — except where a single item is personal data about you specifically, such as your birthday, which goes with your account even if they were the one who added it.
