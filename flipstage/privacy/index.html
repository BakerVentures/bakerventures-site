Privacy Policy — FlipStage

Last updated: October 22, 2026

FlipStage ("the app") is published by Baker Ventures LLC ("we," "us"). FlipStage turns your phone's screen into a two-pane presenting surface — your notes on one side, your slide on the other — for pitching and presenting face-to-face. This policy explains what the app does with your information. It is short because the app does very little with it.

The short version

FlipStage collects nothing. There is no account, no sign-in, and no server of ours for the app to talk to. The photos you import as slides, and the notes you type against them, are used only inside the app on your device and are never transmitted to us or to anyone else. What the app keeps on your device, which depends on the version you are running, and whether your own iCloud backup includes it, are both set out in section 1.

1. What the app uses, and every use of it

The app does not transmit any information to us or to anyone else. The following stays on your device:

What is usedWhere it comes fromWhat it is used for
Photos you choose to importYou pick them from your device's photo library, via the standard system pickerShown as slides in the deck you build, and displayed full-screen during Present mode
Speaker notes you typeYou type them against a slideShown on your side of the screen while you present, so your audience only sees the slide

Your deck, and which version stores it. Your deck is the slides you have imported, their order, and the notes you typed against them. Whether it is written to your device depends on which version of FlipStage you have, and the app's App Store page shows which one that is. Version 1.0 holds the deck in memory only, so it is gone once the app closes. From version 1.0.1 it is written to a file inside the app's own container on your device and read back when you reopen the app. In both versions it stays on your device and is never sent to us or to any third party.

iCloud Backup. FlipStage makes no network requests, so the app itself transmits nothing: it behaves identically in airplane mode. Anything it stores inside its own documents area is, however, part of your device's backup, exactly as other apps' files are: if you have iCloud Backup switched on, iOS includes it. That is Apple holding your own backup under your own iCloud account, not us or a third party receiving anything, and we have no access to it. Which version matters here too. Version 1.0 stores no deck at all and leaves imported-photo copies in temporary storage, which iOS does not back up. From version 1.0.1 the deck file and the photo copies live in the documents area, which iOS does back up. If you would rather they were not, iCloud Backup can be turned off, or FlipStage switched off within it, under Settings, your name, iCloud, Manage Account Storage, Backups.

Photos are kept as a copy. When you pick a photo, the system photo picker hands FlipStage a copy of it, and the app keeps that copy in its own private storage area on your device so it can show it as a slide. Where it is stored: on your device, inside the app's own container, in temporary storage up to version 1.0 and in the documents area from version 1.0.1. Who can see it: no one but you. The copy is not sent to us or shared with anyone. If a copy is no longer on the device when you come back to its slide, the slide shows without its picture and you can import the photo again; the notes you typed on it are not affected. Your original photo in your library is never changed. That is the complete list of what the app touches.

What the app does not collect at all: no name, email address, phone number, or contact detail; no account credentials; no payment or financial information; no location; no contacts, camera, microphone, calendar, or health data; no advertising identifier; no device identifier; no crash reports; no usage or diagnostic telemetry.

No tracking. The app does not track you across apps or websites, does not use an advertising SDK, and does not build or buy a profile about you. Because it collects nothing, there is nothing to sell, and we do not sell or share personal information with anyone, for any purpose, ever.

2. Why FlipStage asks for photo access

FlipStage asks for permission to your photo library only so you can pick images to use as slides. It reads only the specific photos you select in the system picker — it does not scan, upload, or otherwise access the rest of your library, and the photos are never sent to us or to any third party.

3. Third parties

There are none. The app contains no analytics SDK, no crash-reporting SDK, no advertising SDK, no attribution SDK, and no backend of ours. It makes no network requests of any kind — it will function identically in airplane mode. No third party receives your data from this app, because the app sends your data to no one. Your device's own iCloud backup is not an exception to that: the app sends it nothing, iOS copies app files into your backup on your behalf under your own iCloud account, and section 1 says which versions have anything there to copy.

4. Retention, deletion, and withdrawing consent

Retention. We hold nothing, so there is no retention period on our side to describe and no server copy we could produce. On your device: the working copy of each imported photo described in section 1 stays in the app's private storage until you remove that slide, the device reclaims the space, or you delete the app. The deck itself is held in memory only in version 1.0, so it does not outlive the app being open; from version 1.0.1 it is stored alongside those copies and kept on the same terms.

Deletion. Removing a slide clears that slide and its speaker notes from your deck, and there is no undo in any version. The working copy of an imported photo can remain in the app's private storage after its slide is gone, until the device reclaims the space or you uninstall the app. Uninstalling the app removes anything the app itself stored, including those copies and any stored deck, and a device backup taken while they were there stops being restorable once you delete that backup. The original photos you imported remain in your own photo library exactly as before — FlipStage never moves or deletes them there.

Withdrawing consent. Turn off Photos access for FlipStage at any time in your device's Settings. You can still use the app; you just won't be able to import new photos as slides.

5. Children

FlipStage is a general-purpose presenting tool. It is not directed at children and collects no personal data from anyone of any age.

6. Changes to this policy

If the app ever changes what it accesses, stores, or shares, this page will be updated in the same release that makes the change, and the date above will change with it. Where behaviour differs between versions of the app, this page names the version it is describing, so a page you read today is accurate for the version you are actually running rather than only for the newest one.
