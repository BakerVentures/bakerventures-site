Texas mechanics lien deadlines

12 deadlines NoticeGuard models for Texas: what each one counts from, the statute it was read from, and the statute text.

Information, not legal advice. This app reports what the statute says and cites the section it read. It is not legal advice, it does not file anything for you, and it does not tell you what to do.
Verify with the statute or an attorney. Statutes change, and courts reinterpret them. Every deadline in this app comes from a statute read on the date shown next to its citation - that snapshot can go out of date. Missing a mechanics lien deadline can extinguish your claim permanently, so confirm any deadline you are relying on with an attorney licensed in that state before you act on it.

Work out a date for Texas with the calculator

Each deadline, with the statute

Original contractor's lien affidavit (commercial)

Filed with the county clerk, due the 15th day of the 4th month after the month the original contractor's work was completed, terminated, or abandoned.

What it counts from. The 15th day of the 4th month after final completion of the original contract.

Applies to. Original contractor (when you do not tick "This is a residential construction project" and you do not tick "The original contract was signed before January 1, 2022 (an earlier Texas statute governs it, and this app does not cover that one)").

Texas Property Code § 53.052 (a)(1)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-21

“An original contractor claiming the lien must file an affidavit with the county clerk: (1) for projects other than residential construction projects, not later than the 15th day of the fourth month after the month in which the original contractor's work was completed, terminated, or abandoned...”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). The affidavit must be filed by this date to perfect the lien under Chapter 53 - this is the "NECESSARY PROCEDURES" § 53.051 requires to be followed.

Also stated next to this deadline.

  • Confirmed by the statute's own worked structure: the day-of-month is always 15; only the month count and anchor change by role/property-type.
  • The claimant's own contractor tier (original contractor vs. other claimant) does not itself change the month count here - residential vs. commercial does. An original contractor and a commercial-project subcontractor both get the 4th month; both get the 3rd month on a residential project.

Original contractor's lien affidavit (residential)

Filed with the county clerk, due the 15th day of the 3rd month after the month the original contractor's work was completed, terminated, or abandoned - one month earlier than the commercial track.

What it counts from. The 15th day of the 3rd month after final completion of the original contract.

Applies to. Original contractor (when you tick "This is a residential construction project" and you do not tick "The original contract was signed before January 1, 2022 (an earlier Texas statute governs it, and this app does not cover that one)").

Texas Property Code § 53.052 (a)(2)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-21

“for residential construction projects, not later than the 15th day of the third month after the month in which the original contractor's work was completed, terminated, or abandoned.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). The affidavit must be filed by this date to perfect the lien under Chapter 53.

Also stated next to this deadline.

  • Residential construction projects get one month less across the board - confirmed the same one-month-earlier pattern applies to every § 53.052 track (original contractor, other claimant, and even the retainage track, which uses 3 months regardless of property type).

Subcontractor's or supplier's lien affidavit (commercial)

Filed with the county clerk, due the 15th day of the 4th month after the month this claimant last provided labor or materials.

What it counts from. The 15th day of the 4th month after last furnishing (last day on site).

Applies to. Subcontractor, Supplier, Preconstruction service (when you do not tick "This is a residential construction project" and you do not tick "The original contract was signed before January 1, 2022 (an earlier Texas statute governs it, and this app does not cover that one)").

Texas Property Code § 53.052 (b)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-21

“Except as provided by Subsection (c) or (d), a claimant other than an original contractor claiming the lien must file an affidavit with the county clerk not later than the 15th day of the fourth month after the later of: (1) the month the claimant last provided labor or materials; or (2) the month the claimant would normally have been required to deliver the last of specially fabricated materials that have not been actually delivered.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). The affidavit must be filed by this date to perfect the lien under Chapter 53.

Also stated next to this deadline.

  • This app uses only the claimant's own last-furnishing month - the specially-fabricated-materials branch is not modeled; see `limitations`.
  • A separate, retainage-specific version of this filing deadline exists at § 53.052(d) - see the sibling `tx-lien-affidavit-retainage` rule.

Subcontractor's or supplier's lien affidavit (residential)

Filed with the county clerk, due the 15th day of the 3rd month after the month this claimant last provided labor or materials - one month earlier than the commercial track.

What it counts from. The 15th day of the 3rd month after last furnishing (last day on site).

Applies to. Subcontractor, Supplier, Preconstruction service (when you tick "This is a residential construction project" and you do not tick "The original contract was signed before January 1, 2022 (an earlier Texas statute governs it, and this app does not cover that one)").

Texas Property Code § 53.052 (c)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-21

“A claimant other than an original contractor claiming a lien arising from a residential construction project must file an affidavit with the county clerk not later than the 15th day of the third month after the later of: (1) the month the claimant last provided labor or materials; or (2) the month the claimant would normally have been required to deliver the last of specially fabricated materials that have not been actually delivered.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). The affidavit must be filed by this date to perfect the lien under Chapter 53.

Also stated next to this deadline.

  • This app uses only the claimant's own last-furnishing month - the specially-fabricated-materials branch is not modeled; see `limitations`.

Retainage claim's lien affidavit

Filed with the county clerk, due the 15th day of the 3rd month after the month the ORIGINAL CONTRACT (not this claimant's own work) was completed, terminated, or abandoned - the same 3-month period regardless of project type.

What it counts from. The 15th day of the 3rd month after final completion of the original contract.

Applies to. Subcontractor, Supplier, Preconstruction service (when you do not tick "The original contract was signed before January 1, 2022 (an earlier Texas statute governs it, and this app does not cover that one)").

Texas Property Code § 53.052 (d)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-21

“A claimant other than an original contractor claiming a lien for retainage must file an affidavit with the county clerk not later than the 15th day of the third month after the month in which the original contract under which the claimant performed was completed, terminated, or abandoned.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). The affidavit must be filed by this date to perfect a lien for the unpaid retainage specifically.

Also stated next to this deadline.

  • Genuinely different anchor from the sibling ordinary-claim rules above: this one runs from the ORIGINAL CONTRACT's completion (the whole project), not this claimant's own last furnishing - confirmed from the statute's own text.
  • Gets the 3-month (residential-style) period regardless of whether the project is residential or commercial - confirmed no flag condition on this rule.
  • Applies only "to the extent that a claim for unpaid retainage is not included wholly or partly" in the § 53.056 notice - an overlap this app does not check.

Notice of claim for unpaid labor or materials (commercial)

Sent to the owner and the original contractor, due the 15th day of the 3rd month after the month labor or materials were provided. Shown for this claimant's LAST month of work - the statute actually requires one notice per month worked, not a single deadline; see this rule's notes.

What it counts from. The 15th day of the 3rd month after last furnishing (last day on site).

Applies to. Subcontractor, Supplier, Preconstruction service (when you do not tick "This is a residential construction project" and you do not tick "The original contract was signed before January 1, 2022 (an earlier Texas statute governs it, and this app does not cover that one)").

Texas Property Code § 53.056 (a-1)(1)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-21

“For all unpaid labor or materials provided, the claimant must send a notice of claim for unpaid labor or materials to the owner or reputed owner and the original contractor. The notice must be sent: (1) for projects other than residential construction projects, not later than the 15th day of the third month after the month during which: (A) the labor or materials were provided; or (B) the undelivered specially fabricated materials would normally have been delivered...”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). § 53.056(a): "a claimant other than an original contractor must give the notice ... for the lien to be valid" - this is a genuine precondition to lien validity, not merely a payment-priority step.

Also stated next to this deadline.

  • This is a RECURRING, per-month obligation - one notice cycle for every calendar month the claimant worked, each with its own 3-month-later deadline. This app shows only the deadline for the last such month; see `limitations`.
  • The original contractor never owes this notice (§ 53.056(a): "a claimant other than an original contractor") - modeled here by excluding that role entirely.
  • § 53.056(a-2) prescribes an exact "NOTICE OF CLAIM FOR UNPAID LABOR OR MATERIALS" form - not yet templated by this app; see `limitations`.

Notice of claim for unpaid labor or materials (residential)

Sent to the owner and the original contractor, due the 15th day of the 2nd month after the month labor or materials were provided - one month earlier than the commercial track. Shown for this claimant's LAST month of work; see this rule's notes.

What it counts from. The 15th day of the 2nd month after last furnishing (last day on site).

Applies to. Subcontractor, Supplier, Preconstruction service (when you tick "This is a residential construction project" and you do not tick "The original contract was signed before January 1, 2022 (an earlier Texas statute governs it, and this app does not cover that one)").

Texas Property Code § 53.056 (a-1)(2)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-21

“for residential construction projects, not later than the 15th day of the second month after the month during which: (A) the labor or materials were provided; or (B) the undelivered specially fabricated materials would normally have been delivered.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). § 53.056(a): this notice is required "for the lien to be valid" - a genuine precondition, not merely a payment-priority step.

Also stated next to this deadline.

  • This is a RECURRING, per-month obligation - this app shows only the deadline for the claimant's last month of work; see `limitations`.

Notice of claim for unpaid retainage

Sent to the owner and the original contractor, due 30 days after this claimant's own contract (or, if earlier, the original contract) is completed, terminated, or abandoned.

What it counts from. 30 days after final completion of the original contract.

Applies to. Subcontractor, Supplier, Preconstruction service (when you do not tick "The original contract was signed before January 1, 2022 (an earlier Texas statute governs it, and this app does not cover that one)").

Texas Property Code § 53.057 (a-1)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-21

“The claimant must send the notice of claim for unpaid retainage to the owner or reputed owner and the original contractor not later than the earlier of: (1) the 30th day after the date the claimant's contract is completed, terminated, or abandoned; or (2) the 30th day after the date the original contract is terminated or abandoned.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). § 53.057(a): required "for a lien for unpaid retainage to be valid" - a genuine precondition to that specific claim, not merely a payment-priority step.

Also stated next to this deadline.

  • A genuine unit shift from every other rule in this state: this is a simple 30-DAY offset, not a month/day formula - confirmed from the statute's own text.
  • The statute states the earlier of two 30-day counts (from the claimant's own contract, or from the original contract, whichever is earlier) - this app anchors to `finalCompletionOfOriginalContract` alone as the more conservative (never-later-than-true) choice, the same simplification style used elsewhere in this app for "latest/earliest of" facts it does not separately collect.
  • § 53.057(a-2) prescribes an exact "NOTICE OF CLAIM FOR UNPAID RETAINAGE" form - not yet templated by this app; see `limitations`.

Suit to foreclose the lien (original contractor, commercial)

Must be brought within the first anniversary of the original contractor's own lien-affidavit deadline - computed here as the 15th day of the 16th month after the month the original contractor's work was completed, terminated, or abandoned (4 months + 12).

What it counts from. The 15th day of the 16th month after final completion of the original contract.

Applies to. Original contractor (when you do not tick "This is a residential construction project" and you do not tick "The original contract was signed before January 1, 2022 (an earlier Texas statute governs it, and this app does not cover that one)").

Texas Property Code § 53.158 (a)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-21

“Except as provided by Subsection (a-2), suit must be brought to foreclose the lien not later than the first anniversary of the last day a claimant may file the lien affidavit under Section 53.052.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). The statute states plainly: past this date, without a timely-filed suit, "the lien claimant's rights to pursue a suit to foreclose a lien are not revived" (§ 53.158(a-1)).

Also stated next to this deadline.

  • This is genuinely the SAME calendar day, one year later, as the sibling `tx-lien-affidavit-original-contractor-commercial` rule's own deadline - confirmed from the statute's own text ("the first anniversary of the last day a claimant may file the lien affidavit"), which is why this rule reuses the identical anchor and day, adding exactly 12 months.
  • Extendable to a second anniversary of the actual filing date (not the deadline date) by a written agreement recorded with the county clerk before the first deadline expires (§ 53.158(a-2)) - an optional, claimant-initiated extension this app does not model.

Suit to foreclose the lien (original contractor, residential)

Must be brought within the first anniversary of the original contractor's own lien-affidavit deadline - computed here as the 15th day of the 15th month after the month the original contractor's work was completed, terminated, or abandoned (3 months + 12).

What it counts from. The 15th day of the 15th month after final completion of the original contract.

Applies to. Original contractor (when you tick "This is a residential construction project" and you do not tick "The original contract was signed before January 1, 2022 (an earlier Texas statute governs it, and this app does not cover that one)").

Texas Property Code § 53.158 (a)
Official source · statute text retrieved 2026-09-21

“Except as provided by Subsection (a-2), suit must be brought to foreclose the lien not later than the first anniversary of the last day a claimant may file the lien affidavit under Section 53.052.”

If the date is missed (the statute's own terms). Past this date, without a timely-filed suit, the lien claimant's foreclosure rights are not revived (§ 53.158(a-1)).

Also stated next to this deadline.

  • One year after the sibling `tx-lien-affidavit-original-contractor-residential` rule's own deadline - same anchor, same day, +12 months.

Weekend and holiday rollover

NoticeGuard does not model a weekend or holiday extension for Texas. The dates above are the plain count from the date you enter. Check whether a Saturday, Sunday or holiday changes your date.

What this rule set does not cover

  • Every deadline here is Chapter 53 as amended by HB 2237 (2021), and that Act applies BY CONTRACT DATE: "The changes in law made by this Act apply only to an original contract entered into on or after the effective date of this Act. An original contract entered into before the effective date of this Act is governed by the law as it existed immediately before the effective date of this Act, and that law is continued in effect for that purpose" (§ 37), and "This Act takes effect January 1, 2022" (§ 38). The pre-2022 law - different notice deadlines, and a different set of claimants who could lien at all - is NOT modeled by this app. If your original contract was signed before 2022-01-01, tick that box and this app will show you no Texas deadlines, which is the honest answer: do not use the dates below for that contract.
  • Weekend/holiday rollover is genuinely CONFIRMED to apply here - Property Code § 53.003(e), added directly into this chapter in 2021, states plainly that a deadline landing on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday extends to the next non-Saturday/Sunday/legal-holiday day. It is NOT modeled anyway, for a different and more specific reason than most other states' unconfirmed-rollover gaps: this session did not independently verify Texas's statutory legal-holiday enumeration (Government Code § 662.003) against a primary source (the same Angular-app access problem that affected Chapter 53 itself blocked a quick follow-up fetch). Implementing the rollover with a guessed or half-verified holiday list would risk silently wrong dates, which this app treats as worse than showing the honest, un-rolled date. A future session should fetch § 662.003 directly, then wire up a `rollover` block citing § 53.003(e).
  • The month/day anchor for a claimant other than the original contractor (§ 53.052(b)/(c)) is actually "the LATER of (the month the claimant last provided labor/materials) or (the month undelivered specially fabricated materials would normally have been delivered)." This app uses only the claimant's own last-furnishing month (`lastFurnishing`) - the specially-fabricated-materials branch is not modeled, since this app has no fact field for a delivery-that-never-happened date.
  • The § 53.056/§ 53.057 pre-lien notices are, by the statute's own text, RECURRING obligations - one notice cycle per calendar month in which the claimant provided labor or materials, not a single deadline. This app models only the deadline for the claimant's LAST such month (using `lastFurnishing`), the final and most consequential notice in the sequence - not each month's individual notice.
  • The retainage foreclosure-suit deadline is NOT modeled (the ordinary foreclosure-suit rules below cover the general and residential/commercial lien-affidavit tracks, not the separate retainage-affidavit track) - a real, named gap, not an oversight.
  • § 53.024's cap on a subcontractor's lien amount (generally limited to a pro-rata share of the subcontract price) is not modeled - a dollar-amount rule, and this app never collects dollar amounts.
  • § 53.055's requirement that a non-original-contractor claimant send a copy of the filed affidavit to both the owner and the original contractor within 5 days of filing (original contractors need only notify the owner) is not modeled as its own deadline.
  • § 53.107(e)'s owner-to-subcontractor termination/abandonment notice duty does not apply to residential construction projects at all - a real carve-out, not modeled since this app does not generate or track that notice.
  • Only §§ 53.001, 53.023, 53.024, 53.051, 53.052, 53.054, 53.055, 53.056, 53.057, 53.107, and 53.158 have been read (several only as definitional or cross-reference context). The rest of Chapter 53 - including Subchapters H through L (bond-substitution mechanics, §§ 53.171-53.259, and homestead-specific rules) - is not incorporated.
  • This app generates the affidavit claiming lien from § 53.054's required content elements (no prescribed exact form - "contain substantially" the listed items). §§ 53.056(a-2) and 53.057(a-2) separately DO prescribe verbatim notice-letter forms for the pre-lien monthly and retainage notices - those are different documents from the affidavit and are not yet templated; a future session could add them.
