FlirtGym is an iPhone app. It runs practice conversations with AI characters who react to what you actually write, and scores each message you send inside them, so the calibration happens before a real person is on the other end.

What it looks for, and why each one lands badly

These are patterns, not verdicts. Every one of them is something people describe as making a message feel off, and every one of them is fixable in a single rewrite.

The one rule that covers almost all of it

Can she say no to this message comfortably? Not whether she will. Whether the message leaves her an exit that costs her nothing. A message with an exit in it is almost never described as creepy, even when it is forward. A message with no exit is, even when it is polite.

That is why the check gives points back for a line like no worries either way or only if you are up for it. Those four words are not politeness. They are the difference between an offer and a demand, and she can feel which one she is holding.

Questions

Is anything I paste sent anywhere?

No. The check is JavaScript running on your device. There is no request, no logging, no cookie and no storage, and the page works with your connection turned off.

Can a tool really tell whether a message is creepy?

No, and this one does not claim to. It finds patterns that readers consistently report as pushy, and it cannot see intent. A text scanner only ever has the words. So does she, the first time she reads it, which is why the words are worth checking.

It flagged something I did not mean badly. What now?

That is the normal result and it is still worth knowing, because a message is received as it reads rather than as it was meant. When a flag looks unfair, ask the one question underneath all of them: does she have an easy way to say no to what you just sent? If she does, send it.

Does it work on a whole conversation?

It reads one message at a time, which is the thing you can still change. For a whole thread, the reciprocity check measures who is carrying it: share of messages, who asks the questions, who restarts it after a gap.
